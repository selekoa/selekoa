# Key papers pack: same-family audit and dispositions

Two fresh-context auditors of the preparing model family (Claude) checked a recorded sample on 2026-10-28, without the integrator's reasoning, and tried to refute records against the retrieved sources (and, for venues, live proceedings, journal and Crossref pages). Their reports are kept unchanged in [audit/audit-a.md](audit/audit-a.md) and [audit/audit-b.md](audit/audit-b.md). The sample is [audit-sample.json](audit-sample.json) (seed 20261028).

Same-family agreement is not independent corroboration. The cross-family check has not been run.

## Coverage

| Auditor | Sample                                                                                                                                                  | Records | Findings |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------: | -------: |
| A       | 12 random "states" records, every priority-claim quotation (4), every attributed-shaping record (28)                                                    |      43 |       12 |
| B       | 10 random citation records, 8 random authorship records; every citation of the 12 first papers at the threshold (60); versions of 23 papers; 10 persons |    ~110 |       21 |

Both auditors widened their checks beyond the sample where a finding suggested a family of errors (Auditor B: every citation of the AI Risk Management Framework, title matches inside a citing document's own title, matches in encyclopedia links; both: printed names missed by the byline matcher).

## Integrator's changes before the audit

- Two attributed-shaping drafts dropped as too thin to carry an attribution (the Gemini report's generic "builds on" sentence; the Qwen report's hedged remark on which paper first used a chat format).
- Two attributed-shaping quotations turned into labelled paraphrases and one shortened, because the dataset already quotes nearly all of the words allowed from those sources (OpenAI's Frontier Governance Framework, Hibbard's 2006 message, Clancy and Besiroglu's dialogue).
- The priority-claim flag harmonised across the four drafting groups: it marks explicit priority wording only ("the first", "to our knowledge", "any previous", "ever"). A paper calling its own work "new" or "novel" is not flagged; the statement attributes the wording either way.
