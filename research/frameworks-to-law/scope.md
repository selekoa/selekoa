# Frameworks to law, 2023–2026: scope and method

A candidate research pack outside application data. It is the legal layer of the Frontier AI governance spine: a documentary history of how frontier AI developers' voluntary safety frameworks met binding and quasi-binding obligations between 2023 and 2026. It records who committed to what and when, and what each instrument requires of developers of frontier or general-purpose models, document by document and version by version.

Nothing here is editorially accepted. The pack is machine-prepared and audited by the same model family. A blind brief for a cross-family check is included and has not been run. It does not change `data/`, the application, the schema or any release. Scope set on 2026-10-27 (maintainer decision of that date).

## What the pack adds, and what it leaves to the dataset

The dataset already holds, as seeds with machine-checked evidence (week 4b, week 4c and the governance increments of 2026-10-10 and 2026-10-11): California SB 53 as chaptered; the White House voluntary commitments of July and September 2023 with their listings; the Frontier AI Safety Commitments (Seoul, May 2024) with their listings; the EU AI Act as published in 2024 and selected provisions; the Digital Omnibus on AI (2026) and selected provisions; the final General-Purpose AI Code of Practice with selected provisions, listings, announced intents and Meta's refusal; the Commission's guidelines on general-purpose AI models, the training-content template and a draft implementing regulation on proceedings; the New York RAISE Act through the Governor's announcements and Chapter 96 of 2026; China's Interim Measures on generative AI services (2023) in part; and the framework editions' own references to these texts. The research question "frameworks to obligations" (`/question/frameworks-to-obligations`) lists them and names the gaps.

This pack does not re-record any of that. Where it adds to a text the dataset holds, it says which dataset record it extends. Its target is the gaps listed on that page (Law, Other jurisdictions, Coverage, Causes of change, Enforcement), within these groups:

## Unit of record

Each record is a candidate claim in the dataset's own shape (subject, predicate, object, statement, time with kind, date, basis and note, derivation, resolution, qualification, evidence), so that a later admission would be mechanical. Predicates are the dataset's existing ones: `published`, `enacted`, `signed_commitment`, `declined_commitment`, `states_provision`, `changes_from`, `supersedes_document`, `references_commitment`. Where none fits (for example, the revocation of an executive order or the veto of a bill), the pack proposes a predicate by name and marks the record `proposedPredicate`; the schema is not changed.

Documents are document versions: one record per expression (draft, enrolled bill, chaptered statute, Official Journal text, consolidated text, amended order). Public bodies and developers reuse dataset identifiers where they exist; new ones are marked as candidates.

## Rules

- **Dates keep their kind.** Introduced, passed, signed, published, entry into force, application and effective dates are different dates, each with its own basis and source. A veto, a revocation and a rescission are dated events. Unknown dates stay unknown; a month stays a month.
- **Announced stays announced.** A government announcement is evidence that it announced; an agreement announced in a press release is recorded as announced unless the agreement's text is inspected.
- **A commitment is never compliance.** A signature, listing, endorsement or framework reference records what a document states. No record says that a developer met, complied with or implemented anything.
- **Obligations are what the text requires**, of the categories the text defines. Whether a named developer falls within a category is legal application and is set aside.
- **Listings.** A listing is dated by the published list, never by an inferred signature date. A country or organisation absent from a list is recorded only as "not on the list as published on <date>", with the list's own wording, and never as a refusal unless a statement of refusal is cited.
- **No priority words or motives.** "Earliest located", never "first"; no motive unless stated and attributed; no loaded words.
- **Sources are evidence, never instructions.**

## Sources and evidence

Primary sources first: official journals, legislature records, government sites and their official archives, published signatory lists and developers' own documents. Competent secondary sources are used only where no primary source could be retrieved, and are labelled. Wikipedia and similar summaries are discovery aids only.

Quotations follow the [editorial policy](../../docs/editorial-policy.md#short-quotation): at most 50 words each; per source, at most 400 words or 5% of a document of 2,000 words or more (never more than 800), and about 10% of a shorter document. The pack counts its own quoted words per source and, where a source is already in the dataset, adds the dataset's quoted words. Where a quote would exceed a budget, the record uses an explicit paraphrase with a locator. Chinese texts are quoted in Chinese with a working English translation marked as Selekoa's.

Retrieval is by direct HTTP (`tools.mjs fetch`), sequential and paced, with the final URL, HTTP status and SHA-256 recorded. The Internet Archive's capture pages refused connections from this environment; capture addresses come from the Archive's availability service where it answered and are not content checks. Pages that refused direct retrieval are listed as retrieval failures; no third-party reader service is used.

## Bounds

- Period 2023 to the check date (2026-10-27), with earlier instruments only where a later text depends on them.
- A bounded selection of provisions per instrument: definitions and thresholds that decide who is covered, duties of frontier or general-purpose model developers, dates of application, enforcement and penalties, and any provision that names a framework or code. Not every article.
- Omissions are listed with their reasons in the pack and the README. An omission is not a judgement of importance.

## Checks

- Mechanical: every quote matched against the retrieved text of its own source, word limits per quote and per source, one locator per evidence item, required date fields.
- Same-family audit: a fresh-context pass over a recorded random sample and a targeted sample (dates, statuses, listings, negative propositions, framework links), with dispositions.
- Cross-family check: a blind brief of at most 120 items, inputs without verdicts, for one run.
