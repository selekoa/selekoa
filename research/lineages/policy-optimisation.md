# Policy optimisation and reported use

## Question and documentary sequence

What distinguishes a methodological citation from use? [PPO v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06347v1) cites [TRPO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.05477v1) in its trust-region discussion and [Adam](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6980v1) in its bibliography. Its Appendix A reports Adam settings. [Attention Is All You Need v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.03762v1), §5.3, also explicitly reports Adam use.

## Strongest evidence

The Adam hyperparameter tables and Transformer training paragraph support narrow reported-use claims. PPO §§1–2 describe problems with TRPO and motivate a different objective; its citation is not an endorsement or a wholesale adoption claim. Adam's first version names Kingma and Ba with an equal-contribution note. Authorship does not identify a sole inventor.

## Uncertain and unsupported steps

No `reformulates` predicate is introduced on the strength of one PPO/TRPO example. The scoped citation and this dossier preserve the distinction. An Adam citation to a 2015 conference work does not demonstrate use of the inspected 2014 v1 implementation. Later Adam revisions must not silently replace this expression.

## People and institutions

Kingma, Ba and Schulman already have diaspora records. Separate authorship edges make the documents reachable; career movement does not prove that any later employer adopted a method. No new institution is needed.

## Gaps and next research

Adam v1's introduction names AdaGrad and RMSProp as antecedents. This pass inspected that statement but does not add upstream entities without checking the original cited sources and version identities. A later optimisation increment can investigate those sources and the exact PPO/TRPO reformulation in depth. Reported use does not verify implementation or performance.
