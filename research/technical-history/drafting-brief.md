## Common instructions

You are drafting one source group of a candidate research pack in `research/technical-history/` of the Selekoa repository (a source-critical documentary history of machine intelligence). Nothing you write is admitted to the dataset. Read first: `research/technical-history/scope.md`, `research/technical-history/drafts/FORMAT.md`, and the sections "Earliest located texts" and "Short quotation" of `docs/editorial-policy.md`.

- Write exactly one file, `research/technical-history/drafts/<group>.json`, in the draft format. Do not edit any other tracked file, do not commit, do not touch `data/`.
- Retrieve every source yourself with `node research/technical-history/tools.mjs fetch <cacheKey> <url>` (sequential; the tool paces itself). Prefix your cache keys with your group code (`a-`, `b-`, `c-`, `d-`, `e-`) so that groups do not collide. Read with `grep`, `page` and `text`. Copy `sha256` and `httpStatus` from `.local/technical-history/doc/<cacheKey>.json`.
- Quote only what the retrieved text says, ≤ 50 words per quote; run `node research/technical-history/tools.mjs check research/technical-history/drafts/<group>.json` until it reports `no problems`.
- Reuse existing records by ID, never duplicate them: dataset entities in `data/entities.json` (with sources in `data/sources.json`), and the key-papers candidates listed in `.local/th/kp-docs.txt` (full records, with their source URLs and arXiv submission dates, in `.local/th/kp-pack.json`, `documents[]`). For a reused document set `"existing": "dataset"` or `"key-papers"` and add only the dates or passages your records need, from a source you retrieved yourself (you may re-fetch the key-papers source URL).
- Primary texts first: the paper, report, proposal, thesis, proceedings or announcement itself, from the publisher, the proceedings host, the author's or institution's site. Wikipedia and other encyclopedias are discovery aids only and never a source. Secondary scholarship (a history, a survey, a retrospective) is labelled `secondary` and is used for attributed statements ("According to …") or where the primary text could not be retrieved.
- **No priority words.** Never write first, coined, introduced, invented, originated, pioneered, landmark, breakthrough, seminal, famous outside a quotation. Write "earliest located". A text's own priority claim ("we introduce …", "the first …") may be quoted and is marked in the qualification as the text's statement. Disagreements about who named or devised something are recorded as attributed statements side by side (`explicitly_credits` to a person, or `credits_work` to a document), never resolved.
- **Dates keep their kind**: publication, presentation, submission (arXiv), announcement, release, event, claimed. A year stays a year. Give each date its own source passage.
- **Earliest located.** For every concept (family or term) you own: state the search in its `search` block (venues, years, methods, gaps, failures); exactly one `uses_term` record holds `earliest-located-term`; the rest are `use` or `other-sense`. If you find an earlier text after choosing a headline, move the role and add a `corrections` entry (from, to, reason, date).
- Unretrievable books and paywalled papers: list as omissions or retrieval failures, with the URL tried. Do not record a claim from memory. Do not use reader services. web.archive.org capture pages refuse this environment; you may record a capture address from `tools.mjs wayback <url> [yyyymmdd]` as unchecked.
- Aim for the number of claims given for your group (a guide, not a quota: fewer good records beat padding). Every claim has evidence with a locator.
- Finish with a short report: records drafted, sources retrieved, retrieval failures, open questions with recommended answers, anything you were unsure of.
