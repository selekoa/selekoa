## 1 · B1 · Treacherous turn

- **Nick Bostrom, 3 July 2014, UK hardback.** The scanned copyright page identifies the first edition, first impression, ISBN 9780199678112. Chapter 8 uses the term as a heading on p. 116. On p. 118, Bostrom writes “We may call the phenomenon the treacherous turn.” The boxed definition on p. 119 describes a weak AI that cooperates until it can strike and pursue its final values without effective opposition. ## 2 · W02 · Succession by machines would be acceptable or desirable

- **Hans Moravec, February 1979.** _Analog Science Fiction / Science Fact_ 99(2), pp. 59–84, §5, p. 78, left column. After predicting that machine descendants will supersede biological humans, Moravec argues that machine society could preserve what humans value. He asks, “shouldn’t we be happy to see that culture become as capable as possible?” This is a qualified welcome to succession. The 2001 author-site capture also contains the passage. [February 1979 print](https://s3.us-west-1.wasabisys.com/luminist/SF/AN/AN_1979_02.pdf#page=78), [10 July 2001 replay](https://web.archive.org/web/20010710010808id_/http://www.frc.ri.cmu.edu:80/~hpm/project.archive/general.articles/1978/analog.1978.html).
- **Hans Moravec, 1989.** “Human Culture: A Genetic Takeover Underway,” in Christopher G. Langton (ed.), _Artificial Life_, Addison-Wesley, pp. 167–200. On p. 167, Moravec describes the author as someone who “cheerfully concludes the human race is in its last century, and goes on to suggest how to help the process along.” He welcomes the succession described in the chapter. I read the opening in the publisher PDF’s indexed text. The unsigned PDF and library page image were unavailable. The indexed footer says 1988, the author file is headed July 1988, and the library catalogue dates the volume to 1989. The earlier labels do not date a publication of this wording. [Publisher’s chapter record](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.4324/9780429032769-9/human-culture-genetic-takeover-underway-hans-moravec), [Library catalogue](https://archive.org/details/artificiallifepr00inte), [CMU bibliography](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/human-culture-a-genetic-takeover-underway), [Author’s file](https://frc.ri.cmu.edu/~hpm/project.archive/general.articles/1988/takeover.ltx).
- **Marvin Minsky, October 1994.** _Scientific American_ 271(4), pp. 108–113. The magazine facsimile’s text layer gives the closing passage on p. 113, right column, as “Will robots inherit the earth? Yes, but they will be our children.” Minsky presents the machines as our descendants in the context of engineering replacement bodies and brains. The same sentence appears in the revised MIT page self-dated 2 January 1997. [Magazine facsimile](https://www.scribd.com/document/636542764/Will-Robots-Inherit-the-Earth-Marvin-Minsky), [Publisher’s issue metadata](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/will-robots-inherit-the-earth/), [Revised MIT text](https://web.mit.edu/dxh/www/marvin/web.media.mit.edu/~minsky/papers/sciam.inherit.html).

I located an indexed capture of the author’s LaTeX file dated 15 April 2001 at 20:48:44. The capture’s contents and the print remain unverified. ## 3 · W10 · Machine intelligence spreads beyond Earth

**Hans Moravec, February 1979.** In _Analog_ 99(2), §5, pp. 77–78, Moravec predicts the expansion of machine societies in space and that machine descendants will eventually inherit the universe. On p. 82, last paragraph before the references, he imagines a collective intelligence “spreading outwards from the solar system, converting nonlife into mind.” [February 1979 print, p. 82](https://s3.us-west-1.wasabisys.com/luminist/SF/AN/AN_1979_02.pdf#page=82).

- **13 July 2005, “Re: Fighting UFAI.”** In Yudkowsky’s reply, the paragraph beginning “In truth it seems pointless to me anyway” doubts that an agency will understand unfriendly AI or have authority for an extreme response. It does not say whether a clear warning would arrive before danger. [SL4 message](http://sl4.org/archive/0507/11477.html).
- **2006, “Artificial Intelligence as a Positive and Negative Factor in Global Risk.”** In “Rates of Intelligence Increase,” the paragraph beginning “First and foremost” rejects reliance on timely warning. A sudden capability leap could arrive before developers had the years needed to establish safety methods. The MIRI reprint says, “We cannot rely on having distant advance warning before AI is created”. I verified the same wording on p. 20 of the draft dated 31 August 2006, captured 22 October at 10:45:54. The July capture indexed for the draft dated 5 February returned HTTP 429 and has a different digest. The February record date sets the order of this test. The copies read leave the February wording unverified.
