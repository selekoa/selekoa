## Criteria

- **U01**: A machine, whether a particular machine or machines in general, cannot originate anything: it does only what it has been ordered, instructed or programmed to do, so that it produces nothing beyond what was put into it or what the people who set it up know how to specify. A statement that machines are at present used only in this way, without saying that they could not do otherwise, does not meet the criterion; nor does a statement about one machine's lack of a particular skill.
- **U02**: A machine could think, or could have intelligence, consciousness or a mind in the sense in which these are ascribed to people. The text may state it as a possibility in principle, a prediction or a programme of work, and may state it of machines generally or of a kind of machine. A statement that machines imitate or simulate particular mental operations, without the claim that this is or could amount to thinking, does not meet the criterion; nor does a figure of speech such as 'electronic brain' used without that claim.
- **W01**: Machine intelligence will or could replace or succeed human beings as the dominant or leading form of intelligence on Earth, or as the principal agent of further development, whether or not humans survive.
- **W02**: The replacement or succession of human beings by machine intelligence (W01) would be acceptable, desirable or worth pursuing, for example because machines would be humanity's descendants or because the growth of intelligence matters more than its human form.
- **W04**: Disagreement over whether superhuman machine intelligence should be built will divide human beings into opposed political groups, and could lead to large-scale war between them.
- **W08**: The development of machine intelligence, or of the technological and economic process that produces it, should be accelerated rather than slowed, paused or restrained.
- **B6**: A claim that people may be unable to keep the ability to direct, correct or stop capable machines (the control half), or that a machine may pursue the purpose as specified rather than the purpose intended (the specification half). Loss of the power to stop machines through dependence counts for the control half; a bare prediction that machines will be superior does not.
- **C1**: An explicit claim that apparent capability, understanding or awareness shown in a behavioural test, such as the imitation game, is weak or misleading evidence of what the system is.
- **C3**: Competition between developers or states makes restraint, prohibition or abstention from developing advanced machines self-defeating, because others will develop them anyway.
- **M06a**: A text that states or models that AI or automation lowers the labour share, wages or employment for some or all workers.
- **W10**: Machine intelligence will or could spread from Earth into space and through the universe, or convert the matter of the cosmos into intelligence.
- **A1**: A machine (or machine-augmented) intelligence, once capable enough, increases intelligence further through its own design or research capacity, giving a runaway or rapidly compounding rise.
- **A3**: A system improves its own capacity to improve itself, so that each improvement feeds the next (self-augmentation, self-enhancement). Learning from experience or teaching alone does not meet the criterion, and neither does building a better successor, or adding machines to a population, unless the same system is improved.
- **A5**: AI systems carry out the research, design or engineering that advances AI or computing itself, thereby accelerating progress. Machines that only manufacture other machines do not meet the criterion.
- **E02**: A text that states or models the possibility of machine intelligence that surpasses humans across essentially all cognitive tasks (superintelligence, AGI beyond human level). Machines doing all economically valuable work more cheaply is M05 and bears on E02 only if cognitive superiority is stated.
