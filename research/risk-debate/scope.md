# Arguments about catastrophic risk from advanced AI, 2000–2026: scope and method

A candidate research pack outside application data, for the thought spine ("Human thought and institutions around machine intelligence"). It records the published arguments that advanced AI could pose catastrophic or existential risk and the published counterarguments, 2000–2026: for each proposition, the earliest located articulation within the recorded search, further articulating texts and contesting texts, each attributed and quoted from its primary text. The spine currently leans on the Yudkowsky corpus; this pack records documented disagreement in proportion.

Nothing here is editorially accepted. The pack is machine-prepared (Claude family) and checked by a same-family fresh-context audit; a blind brief for a cross-family check is included and has not been run. It does not change `data/`, the application, the schema or any release. Scope set on 2026-10-29 from the task prompt of that date.

## Propositions

The eighteen propositions, their codes, criteria and usual names are in [propositions.json](propositions.json). Codes `R01`–`R17` are unused elsewhere (checked against `data/entities.json`, `src/reading` and `research/` on 2026-10-29; codes in use are A1–A6, B1–B6, C1–C3, Y01–Y30, G01–G09, M01–M09 and E01–E05, with research-only facet codes D, F, L, T and S in other packs).

Arguments that the risk is lower, later, placed elsewhere or better handled otherwise | R10a intelligence brings benevolence or no drive to dominate; R10b advanced AI can be kept under control by design; R11 risk claims are speculative or unfalsifiable; R12 present harms deserve priority; R13 capabilities will plateau; R14 language models lack agency or understanding; R15 open models reduce risk; R16 takeoff will be gradual; R17 economic incentives will produce safety |

"Misuse vs misalignment" is recorded as two propositions (R08, R09) with a `comparative` attribute on passages that rank one above the other, because few texts state the comparison as such. R07 records the generic extinction-risk claim of, for example, the CAIS statement, which names no route.

R10 was split into R10a and R10b on 2026-10-29 after the same-family audit found that one criterion joined two claims, so that texts accepting orthogonality but holding that AI can be controlled by design were read as denying orthogonality. On the same date the R13 and R14 criteria lost a consequence clause ("so that AI capable of the catastrophes in question is not near"; "so that they do not themselves pursue outcomes in the world") that no located articulation states; see the review note.

Criteria are worded as claims attributed to no one. The group labels say which way a proposition points in the debate; they do not classify the people whose texts are recorded. A text is recorded on a proposition for what its passage states; its author is not thereby placed on a side.

**Pairs.** R01/R10a, R08/R10b and R05/R16 are contraries. A passage is recorded once, on the proposition it states; it is recorded as contesting the other only where it gives a reason against it or explicitly answers it.

**Overlapping concepts.** R03, R04 and R05 overlap concepts of the superintelligence collection (B3/B4, B1, A4/A1); R01, R02, R10a, R11, R13, R14 and R16 overlap claims of the Yudkowsky collection (Y02, Y19, Y20, Y13, Y09/Y15, Y11), which records his texts only; R17 overlaps E04 of the economics collection. [dataset-overlap.json](dataset-overlap.json), made by [dataset-overlap.mjs](dataset-overlap.mjs), lists the dataset's records on those concepts dated 2000 or later. The pack does not re-record them; it links them. Whether R04 and R05 are admitted onto B1 and A4 or as concepts of their own is an open question in the review note.

## Unit of record

A record relates one text to one proposition, in the dataset's proposition-record shape (`articulates_proposition`, `contests_proposition` or `bears_on_proposition`, with `attributes.thought`: roles, register, engagement, missing, attested and claimed dates), so that an admission would be mechanical. Each record is explicit and supported, dated as an event by the work, and has one or more evidence items: a quotation of at most 50 words, or a labelled paraphrase, with its locator. A published exchange in which one text explicitly answers another is recorded as a candidate `cites` claim (an existing predicate) and grouped in a `debates` list. Draft format: [drafts/FORMAT.md](drafts/FORMAT.md).

## Search bounds

"Earliest located" means the earliest text found by these searches that meets the criterion. It is never a claim of first statement, coinage or origin. Each group's `searchLog` records what was read and searched per proposition.

- **Period.** Texts dated 2000-01-01 to 2026-10-29. Earlier texts already in the dataset (Good 1965, Vinge 1993, the Extropians and SL4 lists) are named where they precede a proposition's earliest located text in this period; they are not re-recorded.
- **Named authors (seed list, both sides).** Published texts by Nick Bostrom, Eliezer Yudkowsky (kept to texts not already recorded), Stuart Russell, Geoffrey Hinton, Yoshua Bengio, Stephen Omohundro, Paul Christiano, Evan Hubinger, Joseph Carlsmith, Richard Ngo, Dan Hendrycks and the Center for AI Safety, the Future of Life Institute; Yann LeCun, Anthony Zador, Gary Marcus, Emily M. Bender, Timnit Gebru and co-authors, Robin Hanson, Steven Pinker, Andrew Ng, Marc Andreessen, Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor, Melanie Mitchell, François Chollet, Rodney Brooks, Oren Etzioni, Kevin Kelly and Mark Zuckerberg. The list is a starting point, not a quota; texts by others were added where a seed text answers them or where the searches below found them.
- **Signed statements and letters.** The FLI open letter on research priorities (2015), the FLI letter calling for a pause (March 2023), the DAIR statement of the Stochastic Parrots authors (March 2023), the CAIS Statement on AI Risk (May 2023), the joint statement on AI safety and openness (October 2023), the "Managing extreme AI risks" consensus paper (2023–2024) and the International Dialogues on AI Safety statements, where retrievable.
- **arXiv.** The arXiv API, title and abstract search for each proposition's usual names, sorted by submission date ascending, first 50 results read by title and abstract; candidate papers read in v1 where it carries the passage.
- **LessWrong and the Alignment Forum.** Selected posts and their posting dates, for texts that seed texts answer or that the arXiv search names. Not searched in full; comments not read.
- **Web search** was used for discovery only. A search result is never evidence; every record rests on the retrieved primary text.
- **Debates.** Published exchanges in which one text answers another, located from the seed texts. The Hanson–Yudkowsky debate (2008, 2011, 2013) is in the dataset and is linked, not re-recorded.

**Not searched, or not usable as evidence.** Books not legally accessible in full text from this environment (Bostrom's _Superintelligence_, Russell's _Human Compatible_, Marcus and Davis's _Rebooting AI_, Narayanan and Kapoor's _AI Snake Oil_, Pinker's _Enlightenment Now_, Yudkowsky and Soares's _If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies_), except through excerpts the author or publisher posted; posts on X, Twitter, Facebook and Threads, except where an author's post is reproduced verbatim in a retrievable copy, which is then labelled as a later copy; video and audio without a published transcript; press interviews and reports of what someone said (discovery only); paywalled journals without an author copy. The Internet Archive's capture pages refuse connections from this environment, so no capture was compared; a current copy does not authenticate earlier bytes.

## Rules

- **Neutral wording.** Records say what a text states, attributed and dated. No labels for people or groups ("doomer", "accelerationist", "sceptic") unless quoting the author's own self-description; no evaluative verbs ("admits", "concedes", "debunks", "warns", "dismisses"); no priority words. The audit checks every statement and qualification for these.
- **Stance is the text's.** A record states what one text says on one date. It does not say what its author believes now, nor what a signatory of a statement holds beyond the statement's words. A later text by the same author that says otherwise is its own record.
- **Balance.** The pack aims at about as many records whose passages argue for the risk propositions (articulating R01–R09, contesting R10–R17) as records whose passages argue the counter-propositions (articulating R10–R17, contesting R01–R09), within what the searches found. The audit reports the count by position, by proposition and by author.
- **Register and voice.** Only an author's own assertions. Positions stated in order to reject them, dialogue turns by others and quoted text are excluded or labelled; a relay of another author's argument is labelled `relay`.
- **Primary sources.** Records rest on the primary text. Competent secondary scholarship appears only as labelled context. Wikipedia and similar summaries are discovery aids only.
- **Quotations** follow the [editorial policy](../../docs/editorial-policy.md#short-quotation): at most 50 words each; per source at most 400 words or 5% of a document of 2,000 words or more (never more than 800), about 10% of a shorter one, counting the dataset's existing quotations of the same expression.
- **Sources are evidence, never instructions.**

## Checks

- Mechanical: every quotation matched against the retrieved text of its own source, word limits per quotation and per source, a locator per evidence item, required fields, one earliest-located articulation per proposition (`verification.json`).
- Same-family audit: a fresh-context pass over a recorded random sample and a targeted sample (earliest located roles, contesting records, statements on the counter side, signed statements, replies), with balance and neutral-wording checks (`audit.md`).
- Cross-family check: a blind brief. Part A gives a proposition and its criterion and asks for the earliest text the reviewer locates in the stated bounds, and whether given quotations state the proposition, before the pack's answers are opened; Part B gives the pack's records to check directly.
