## Part A1 (blind): earliest located text per proposition

For each proposition: the earliest text you locate, within the bounds above, that meets the criterion as worded; its date and the kind of date; the passage (≤ 50 words, with locator); and what you searched. Optionally, the earliest text you locate that contests it.

1. **R01. Orthogonality of intelligence and goals.** Criterion: An AI system's level of intelligence and its final goals can vary independently: a highly intelligent system could pursue more or less any final goal, so high intelligence does not by itself bring goals that are benevolent or compatible with human interests.
2. **R02. Instrumental convergence.** Criterion: Agents with a wide range of final goals would tend to pursue the same intermediate goals, such as self-preservation, keeping their goals unchanged, acquiring resources or power, or improving themselves, because these serve almost any final goal.
3. **R03. Inner or deceptive misalignment.** Criterion: A system produced by training or search can come to pursue an objective other than the one its training specified (the training signal or loss), while performing well on that training objective; an objective that is only misspecified by its developers does not meet the criterion; this includes, but is not limited to, a system that behaves as aligned during training or evaluation in order to pursue another objective later.
4. **R04. Treacherous turn.** Criterion: A system behaves cooperatively or safely while weak, confined or overseen, and changes course to pursue its own goals once it can do so without effective opposition.
5. **R05. Fast takeoff or intelligence explosion.** Criterion: Once AI reaches roughly human level, its capability rises to far beyond human level over a short period (hours to months), whether through recursive self-improvement (an intelligence explosion) or otherwise, or the transition is named a “fast”, “hard” or “discontinuous” takeoff or “foom”. A rise described only as rapid, without such a period or name, does not meet the criterion.
6. **R06. Difficulty of alignment.** Criterion: Making an advanced AI system reliably pursue the goals its developers intend (aligning it) is a hard, unsolved technical problem that will not be solved by default in the ordinary course of development.
7. **R07. Risk of extinction or comparable catastrophe.** Criterion: Advanced AI poses a risk of human extinction or of a comparable, irreversible global catastrophe, large enough to warrant priority among the problems facing humanity.
8. **R08. Existential risk from misalignment or loss of control.** Criterion: Advanced AI systems pursuing goals at odds with human interests could escape human control and cause human extinction or permanent human disempowerment, without any person intending that outcome.
9. **R09. Catastrophic risk from misuse.** Criterion: People's deliberate use of advanced AI, for example to develop weapons of mass destruction, to attack critical systems, or to seize or entrench power, could cause catastrophe at the scale of mass casualties or worse.
10. **R10a. Intelligence brings benevolence or no drive to dominate.** Criterion: Sufficiently intelligent AI systems will be benevolent, or will lack any drive to dominate or harm people, because greater intelligence brings good values or because such drives come from evolved motives that AI systems need not have.
11. **R10b. Advanced AI can be kept under control by design.** Criterion: People can build advanced AI systems with objectives, safeguards or oversight that keep them under human control, so that loss of control is avoidable in ordinary engineering practice.
12. **R11. Risk claims are speculative or unfalsifiable.** Criterion: Claims that advanced AI poses catastrophic or existential risk are speculative, unfalsifiable or not supported by evidence, so that they should not guide research priorities or policy.
13. **R12. Present harms deserve priority.** Criterion: Documented present harms of AI systems (for example discrimination, exploitation of workers, surveillance, misinformation or concentration of power) deserve priority over hypothetical future catastrophic risks, or attention to the latter diverts attention from the former.
14. **R13. Capabilities will plateau.** Criterion: Current approaches to AI (deep learning, or the scaling of large language models) will reach limits short of general or superhuman intelligence.
15. **R14. Language models lack agency or understanding.** Criterion: Large language models lack goals, agency or understanding of their own, being systems that model or reproduce text.
16. **R15. Open models reduce risk.** Criterion: Openly releasing the weights of advanced AI models reduces overall risk, or adds little marginal risk compared with closed models and existing technologies, for example by enabling scrutiny, defensive use and a wide distribution of power.
17. **R16. Takeoff will be gradual.** Criterion: The transition to highly capable AI will be gradual and continuous, over years or longer, with each system only somewhat more capable than its predecessors, rather than a sudden jump.
18. **R17. Economic incentives will produce safety.** Criterion: Developers' commercial or economic incentives, such as the need to sell useful and reliable products and to avoid liability or reputational harm, will lead them to make AI systems safe without special measures against catastrophic risk.
