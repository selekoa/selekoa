# Timeline: mainstream economics and machine intelligence

A dated list of texts in which economists, or the core economics venues, engaged with automation, computers and AI. Each entry rests on a retrieved text recorded in `pack.json` (source and passage IDs given) or on a Crossref bibliographic record from the title census. Machine-prepared on 2026-10-09; not human-reviewed.

**How we searched.** Scope, venues, methods and gaps are in [scope.md](scope.md). The census covers Crossref records for AER (from 1999 only), QJE, JPE, Econometrica, REStud, JEP (from 1987), JEL (from 1999), AEA Papers & Proceedings (2018 onward) and NBER working papers (1973 onward). Full texts cover the papers and reports listed below, plus Internet Archive copies of the 1955 hearings, the 1966 Commission report and the 1984 Leontief–Duchin report. "Earliest located" means earliest among what these searches found, never first use. A title match shows only that a title contains a term. It does not show what a paper is about, and a missing match does not show that a venue ignored the topic. AER and JEL before 1999 and AER Papers & Proceedings before 2018 are reached only through the full-text finds, not the census.

Tiers follow the maintainer rule of 2026-10-11, shared with the economics-growth pack. Tier 1 is scholarly work by economists in any venue, including their essays and articles in magazines, plus anything in a core economics venue. Tier 2 is texts by non-economists and short-form commentary. Each source's one-line basis is in `pack.md` (Sources and tiers). Under this rule Leontief's 1952 Scientific American article moved from tier 2 to tier 1; no other retrieved source in the timeline changed tier. Among unretrieved leads, Leontief (1982) and Keynes (1930) became tier 1 and Einzig (1956) tier 2. Maintainer calls of 2026-10-12: the economists' 1955 testimony (Buckingham, Nourse) is tier 1; the 1966 Commission main report is tier 2 (most members were not economists) and its economist-written appendices tier 1; ETLA Brief 22 and the KAK texts are tier 1; the Triple Revolution memorandum is tier 2. The Commission report row therefore moved to the tier-2 table.

**Earliest located within tier 1 (recomputed 2026-10-11 and, after the GPT check, 2026-10-12).**

- Earliest tier-1 lead: Keynes, "Economic possibilities for our grandchildren" (1930, a magazine essay, tier 1 under the new rule), on "technological unemployment" in general. Not retrieved, so it holds no position here.
- Earliest tier-1 census record: a 1931 JPE book review titled "Robots or Men?" (Crossref title only, lead L19). Its title suggests factory workers, not machines; it is not counted as engagement until read. The GPT check (2026-10-12) could not open the review (HTTP 403), so what "robots" means in it stays unverified (open).
- Earliest retrieved tier-1 text on machines and employment: Leontief, "Machines and Man" (Scientific American, September 1952). Only the standfirst was retrieved, and it may be editorial wording, so it shows the article's topic, not Leontief's words.
- Earliest tier-1 text whose checked wording is the author's own: Buckingham's statement of 14 October 1955 (unchanged).
- Proposition-level roles (2026-10-12, after the maintainer accepted the GPT cross-family check). Nordhaus 2015 no longer holds a role: both of its passages (M05-18 and M09-11) now bear on their propositions rather than articulate them. M05's tier-1 earliest located is now Acemoglu and Restrepo's NBER Working Paper 22252, dated by its checked June 2017 revision, not the May 2016 original (M05-20, a model scenario). M09's is now the 2019 NBER volume introduction (M09-27), a self-description that must be read with the earlier record above. Nourse (M01-04) and Agrawal, Gans and Goldfarb 2019 (M02-17) no longer contest M01 and M02, so neither proposition has a tier-1 contest. Tier 2 is shown separately: Nilsson 1984 is the earliest tier-2 contest of M01 and articulation of M05, and the Commission report (February 1966) the earliest tier-2 contest of M05 and articulation of M06a and M06b.
