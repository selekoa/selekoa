# Library list: texts not retrieved

Prepared on 2026-10-11 under maintainer decision D4. Each entry gives the full APA 7 reference, why the text matters to this pack, and what exactly to check in it. References with a DOI were checked against Crossref on 2026-10-11 (an end page that Crossref lacks is marked); book references follow Internet Archive catalogue records and should be checked against the title page. Lead IDs (L01–L21; L21 added on 2026-10-12) and source IDs refer to `pack.json`; propositions M01–M09 are listed in `pack.md`.

Nothing here is evidence until the text is read and the quotation is checked against it. A text read from this list enters the pack as a new source with its own tier basis, date and locators, and goes through the same audit and cross-family check (D6).

## A. Read first: the pre-2015 record (for an article introduction)

1. **Simon, H. A. (1965). _The shape of automation for men and management_. Harper & Row.** (L01, tier 1)
   - _Why:_ the strongest pre-2015 candidate for an economist stating M05 in his own voice. The pack has his wording only as relayed by Nordhaus (2015; M05-41, M05-42) and Armer (1966; M05-06). If it holds, it would be the earliest located tier-1 own-voice articulation of M05, where the pack now has only a model scenario (Acemoglu and Restrepo, NBER w22252 as revised June 2017, M05-20, since 2026-10-12; previously Nordhaus 2015, M05-18, now bears-on). Follow-up lead confirmed on 2026-10-12: the GPT check could not read the original either, so Simon's own wording stays unverified. It also bears on M09: Nordhaus calls the modern macroeconomic literature on the Singularity "remarkably little" fifty years after this book.
   - _Check:_ pp. xii–xiii, for the sentence Nordhaus quotes ("in our time, computers will be able to do anything a man can do") and p. xiii for "a continuing, but not greatly accelerated, rise in productivity"; the exact wording and whether either sentence is conditional. Chapter I, "The long-range economic effects of automation": whether Simon argues from comparative advantage that full employment survives, which would make his text contest M05 even while forecasting full machine capability. Whether the chapter is a reprint and, if so, its first date.

2. **Leontief, W. (1983). Technological advance, economic growth, and the distribution of income. _Population and Development Review_, _9_(3), 403–410. https://doi.org/10.2307/1973315** (L03, tier 1; end page 410 from a search lead, Crossref gives only p. 403)
   - _Why:_ the text most often cited for Leontief's horse analogy (human labour going the way of horses after tractors), the most direct pre-2015 economist's statement bearing on M05 and M06a.
   - _Check:_ whether the horse analogy is in this article or only in the 1982 Scientific American piece (L04), with exact wording and page; whether it says labour "will" be displaced or "could" be; whether it proposes income policy (M06b). The end page (Crossref gives only p. 403).

3. **Leontief, W. W. (1982). The distribution of work and income. _Scientific American_, _247_(3), 188–204. https://doi.org/10.1038/scientificamerican0982-188** (L04, tier 1 since 2026-10-11)
   - _Why:_ an economist's magazine article, now tier 1. A probable earlier home of the horse analogy (M05, M06a) and of proposals for distributing work and income (M06b).
   - _Check:_ the horse analogy's wording and page; whether computers or "intelligent" machines are named; any policy proposal; whether the 1983 article repeats the wording (the earlier dated wording decides earliest located).

4. **Leontief, W. (1952). Machines and man. _Scientific American_, _187_(3), 150–160. https://doi.org/10.1038/scientificamerican0952-150** (source `leontief1952`, tier 1 since 2026-10-11; only the standfirst was retrieved)
   - _Why:_ now the earliest located tier-1 text on machines and employment in the timeline, but only through a standfirst that may be editorial wording (M05-00, reported voice).
   - _Check:_ whether the standfirst's question ("will they cause widespread technological unemployment?") is Leontief's; how he classes automatic control and computers (M01); his own answer on unemployment and its premises (M05 contest or articulation; M06a); any passage on machines doing mental work.

5. **Simon, H. A. (1960). _The new science of management decision_. Harper.** (L02, tier 1)
   - _Why:_ the 1965 book's catalogue note says the 1960 edition was published under this title. If the 1960 text already holds the forecast, it is the earlier dated wording.
   - _Check:_ whether the 1965 wording in item 1 (or a forecast like it) is already present; page; any change between editions.

6. **Einzig, P. (1956). _The economic consequences of automation_. Secker & Warburg.** (L06, tier 2 by decision D2)
   - _Why:_ an early book-length treatment of automation economics, reviewed in the JPE (item 7). Tier 2 (a financial journalist), so context only, but it shows what economists were reviewing in the 1950s.
   - _Check:_ whether it treats automation as ordinary mechanisation or as a new category (M01); its view on long-run unemployment (M05, M06a); any mention of computers or "electronic brains".

7. **Shultz, G. P. (1958). [Review of the book _The economic consequences of automation_, by P. Einzig]. _Journal of Political Economy_, _66_(6), 560. https://doi.org/10.1086/258120** (L11, tier 1, core venue)
   - _Why:_ the earliest located core-journal title containing "automation" in the census; a tier-1 economist's view of the 1950s automation debate.
   - _Check:_ the reviewer's verdict; whether he treats automation as distinct from earlier technical change (M01); any statement about how economists were treating the topic (M09).

8. **Buckingham, W. S. (1961). _Automation: Its impact on business and people_. Harper.** (L07, tier 1)
   - _Why:_ the book-length version of the argument in Buckingham's 1955 statement, which holds the earliest-located roles for M01 (M01-02), M05 (contesting, M05-03) and M06a (M06a-01).
   - _Check:_ whether the 1961 wording repeats, strengthens or retracts the 1955 claims on "brainwork" machinery (M01), long-run unemployment (M05) and displacement (M06a). Later wording does not displace the 1955 roles but can qualify them.

9. **Dunlop, J. T. (Ed.). (1962). _Automation and technological change_. Prentice-Hall.** (L08, tier 1 as edited by an economist; chapters tiered by author)
   - _Why:_ an American Assembly volume collecting economists' views at the height of the 1960s automation debate; bears on M01, M05 and M09.
   - _Check:_ the contents page and each author's standing (each chapter needs its own tier); chapters on employment and on computers; any statement on whether economists treated automation as a research topic.

10. **Leontief, W., & Duchin, F. (1986). _The future impact of automation on workers_. Oxford University Press.** (L05; book version of `leontief-duchin1984`)
    - _Why:_ the pack quotes the 1984 NSF report (M02-04 to M02-06, M06a-07, M08-01, M09-07). The book is the version most readers will cite.
    - _Check:_ whether the passages quoted from the 1984 report survive unchanged in the book, and their pages; whether the 10% labour-saving figure and the clerical-share scenario are restated.

11. **Keynes, J. M. (1930). Economic possibilities for our grandchildren. _The Nation and Athenaeum_, _48_(2–3).** Pages to verify. Reprinted in J. M. Keynes, _Essays in persuasion_ (Macmillan, 1931). (L18, tier 1 under the 2026-10-11 rule)
    - _Why:_ context for M05 and M09 ("technological unemployment"); it does not concern machine intelligence and cannot hold a role for it.
    - _Check:_ the exact wording and pages of the passage on technological unemployment in the 1930 magazine printing, and the issue numbers and dates of its two parts.

## B. Journal versions and full texts behind publisher access

These are tier-1 texts that the pack holds only as an abstract page or as a different version. Read the full text to check that the quoted abstract or working-paper wording stands in the published version, and to look for passages the abstract cannot show.
