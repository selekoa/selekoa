# Evaluation Institutions I

This exploratory increment asks how independent frontier-model evaluation developed institutionally, and what documented relationships connect ARC Evals, METR, developers and public safety institutes. It covers selected developments in 2022–2024 and a government response published in January 2025. It is not a benchmark inventory, a history of all evaluators, or a claim that these actors uniquely originated independent evaluation.

All new application claims are seeds, not editorially accepted. Evidence is machine checked. The map and dossiers are exploratory projections, not human-curated interpretations. The independent audit and the pending human sample are described in [the review plan](../review/evaluation-institutions-i-spot-check.md). Original records, including `arc-evals` and `rsp-credits-arc-2023`, are preserved.

## Concrete criticism and response

**EI-N9 — UK access oversight.** The Commons Science, Innovation and Technology Committee's [May 28, 2024 report](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5804/cmselect/cmsctech/38/report.html), chapter 6, paragraphs 88–92, raises a conditional concern about access to unreleased models and requests disclosure of tests and refusals. This is primary evidence of parliamentary criticism. Named-lab allegations in paragraph 88 are attributed to reporting, not independently established by the committee.

The [government response](https://committees.parliament.uk/publications/46145/documents/230927/default/), response to recommendations 21–26, printed pp9–10 (PDF pp12–13), says pre- and post-deployment tests occurred, invokes confidentiality and declines to name developers still negotiating access. It was received **November 25, 2024** and published **January 10, 2025**. Both statements are supported as attributed positions; whether particular labs refused particular evaluations remains unresolved. The later response does not settle every earlier concern. Do not encode a blanket “no access” claim, a named refusal or regulatory capture from these sources.

## Version discipline and unresolved questions

- The September and December 2023 METR pages contain retrospective text about an April 2024 appointment. Their edit dates are unknown. Legal formation, staff transfer and Christiano's decision need archived expressions and/or incorporation records before precise dating.
- The pilot announcement displays July 31, 2023 despite August 1 in its URL. The displayed date is recorded; no silent normalisation.
- The original RSP's advice, commitments and reported prior evaluation are separate. A promising methodology or a written policy is not evidence of comprehensive adoption, effectiveness or compliance.
- The sampled records do not establish the complete donor base, all government contracts, or the terms governing evaluation publication. Funding, shared personnel and access alone do not prove influence, ideology or capture.
- Direct retrieval of the BIS government attachment failed. The author-hosted dated comment establishes its text; receipt and policy uptake need a separate docket investigation.
- This increment does not cover every evaluator, public institute, model release or subsequent institutional renaming. Historical UK/US AISI names are retained for the documented events.
