# Editorial policy

## Atomic claims and sources

Every substantive Selekoa claim must have direct inspectable evidence. A record should make one falsifiable assertion. Use the closest and strongest documentary source appropriate to the exact proposition, not a summary that merely points toward it.

Prefer, where available:

- Original papers and pinned versions.
- Contemporaneous institutional documents and archived historical webpages.
- Original posts, mailing-list messages and forum posts.
- Filings, regulatory decisions and court records.
- Official announcements for the fact that the announcement occurred.
- First-person records, scoped to what the person can document.
- Authoritative archival material.

Competent secondary scholarship and high-quality reporting remain admissible when they establish a proposition through independent investigation, synthesis or reporting, or when a suitable original source is unavailable. Competent datasets may supply attributed evidence with exact version, locator and terms records. Secondary evidence is not a fallback truth tier, and primary sources are not automatically truer. Label the source's role relative to each claim.

Wikipedia and comparable tertiary/general-reference summaries may be used for discovery or orientation, but must never be the evidentiary source for an admitted Selekoa claim. General contextual links in explanatory UI may point to useful external resources; keep them visually and semantically distinct from claim evidence. This source standard does not itself alter existing factual records or retrospective research assessments.

A corporate announcement is primary evidence that the company announced something. It is not independent verification that money was paid, a contract remains exclusive, a safeguard works, or a planned facility operates. Scope the claim accordingly. Dates and qualifications must be visible alongside evidence.

Each evidence record requires a stable claim/source reference, a specific section/page/data locator, a short quote or clearly labelled paraphrase, support role, source role, check date and verification state. Prefer a stable version URL for mutable documents; retain canonical and evidence URLs separately. Where feasible, preserve the exact historical expression, version, archival URL, original language and date precision, together with disagreement and retrieval limits. Separate event, publication, revision, capture and check dates; unknown dates remain unknown. An archive link or current copy alone does not authenticate earlier bytes. Keep verbatim excerpts minimal and track reuse conditions per source. Do not redistribute whole publications.

### Short quotation

Short verbatim quotation is permitted from publicly published documents when it is attributed, precisely located and used for commentary on, or identification of, what the document says. Each quote is at most 50 words; prefer the shortest passage that carries the point. Quoted words are counted per source document across the dataset, and each edition or version recorded as its own source counts separately; a passage quoted for several claims counts once. From a document of 2,000 words or more, quote at most 400 words or 5% of the document, whichever is larger, and never more than 800. From a shorter document, quote at most about 10% of it; where 10% would leave a misleading fragment, one complete sentence of up to 25 words may be quoted instead, as the only passage quoted from that document. There is no cap on the number of quotes. Each source with quotes records its approximate length (`documentWords`: the count and how it was made) so the limit can be checked; validation enforces the limits from schema 2.8. (Maintainer decision of 2026-10-15, following the [policy review](policy-review-2026-10-15.md#1-quote-limits); it replaces the flat 400-word cap approved on 2026-10-03, which had replaced an earlier cap of about five passages and 250 words per document. Quotes admitted under earlier caps stay as they are.) Never reproduce tables, figures or whole sections. Quote where the exact wording is materially more precise than a paraphrase — thresholds, conditions, who decides, named predecessors, effective dates — and keep the paraphrase otherwise. A quote must match the cited edition, not merely the file currently served; record which expression was checked.

A source's reuse note describes Selekoa's practice for that source, not the publisher's terms. Publisher terms that forbid quotation override this policy.

## Interpretation

Do not label institutions or people with inferred ideological or social memberships. Record self-description, explicit terminology, documents, institutional participation, disagreement, employment, funding, citation and documented intellectual credit individually. Labels such as “doomer”, “accelerationist” or “EA” must never be assigned from proximity or model inference. Treat contested terminology historically and source-critically; coverage of effective altruism, alignment/safety or accelerationist currents is not culture-war classification. Citation is not agreement. Chronology is not influence. Similar vocabulary is not transmission. Preserve reformulation, convergence, independent invention and unresolved similarity as distinct analytical possibilities.

Use two separate claim dimensions. Derivation is explicit when a source states the proposition, inferred when it follows from reasoning recorded in `inferenceRationale`, or posed when Selekoa frames the proposition as a question that sources take positions on (every assessment, and any unresolved question that is not an inference). A posed record is never presented as stated in a source. Resolution is supported, contested or unresolved. Neither depends on whether evidence is primary or secondary. No numeric confidence score is used.

Supported means support for the exact scoped proposition, not objective truth or absence of undiscovered contradictions. Contested records retain supporting and contradictory evidence together. Unresolved questions carry context evidence only: evidence that supports or contradicts the proposition makes a record supported or contested, and validation enforces this. A resolved objection may remain attached to a supported record, with the adjudication explained in its qualification. Do not delete opposing evidence to simplify a label.

A proposition states the substantive question, never “sources disagree whether…”. Mark contested only when opposing passages address the same proposition; company descriptions of design or intent and critics’ statements of risk or degree are context unless they directly assess the same question.

Evidence roles describe support, contradiction or context. Source counts describe coverage, not independence or corroboration. Derivation, resolution and human review must never be inferred from counts or source-role labels.

## Earliest located texts

A collection that orders texts on a proposition names its earliest text with the headline "Earliest located: …". The phrase means the earliest text that the collection's recorded searches found and that meets the proposition's criterion. It is not a claim that no earlier text exists. The earliest located use of a proposition's usual name is a separate role, because a text can use a name, for example as a title, without stating the proposition.

- **No priority words.** Never describe a text or person as the first to state a proposition, or say that they coined, originated, introduced or invented it, unless a competent authority says so. Then attribute the statement to that authority ("According to …"), cite it as evidence of its own, and keep it apart from the earliest-located record. A negative statement that blocks a misreading ("not evidence of coinage") is allowed.
- **Scope once.** Each collection states its scope once, in a "How we searched" note: the venues and years searched, the methods, the known gaps and the retrieval failures. Records do not repeat it; their qualifications say only what is particular to them.
- **Two dates where they differ.** A record carries the date its work is dated by. Where the quoted wording survives only in a later copy (an archive capture, a reprint marked as changed, a later electronic edition or a relay), the record also carries the date from which that wording is attested, and both dates are shown. The attested date decides which text is earliest located; a text known only from an undated later copy cannot be. An earlier claimed date that does not date the wording, such as a lecture, talk or draft, is shown as claimed.
- **Later finds are corrections.** When a search finds an earlier text that meets the criterion, it becomes the earliest located text. The earlier headline stays on record as a correction, with the reason and the date of the change.
- **Register.** Label fiction, satire, dream framing and relays of another author's argument. A passage in fiction or satire documents what the work says, not its author's view.
- **Order is not influence.** A sequence of texts on a proposition establishes no transmission between them. Most developed is an editorial judgement among the located texts, not a measure.

## Machine-first, risk-based editorial workflow

Exploratory research uses machine-first quality assurance. Routine human spot-checks are not mandatory for each new increment, and manual inspection of every exploratory record is not a release condition. Review effort follows use and risk. This policy does not authorize publication, deployment or a change to repository visibility.

| Use                                               | Required review and limits                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Exploratory, source-backed records                | Require passage-level checking of original evidence, precise locators, version/date qualifications and reproducible validation of records, references and generated artifacts. Run a bounded fresh-context machine audit combining random sampling and targeted consequential or risky claims. Record actual coverage, disagreements and retrieval failures. Keep seeds and actual evidence-verification states visible; machine agreement is neither historical certainty nor human acceptance. |
| Curated historical interpretations                | A human inspects the selected claims, original source paths and narrative framing, including omissions, connective language and unusually prominent placement. Record the reviewed selection, version, date, reviewer and limits. A generated dossier is an exploratory projection until that inspection is explicitly recorded; selection or presentation alone is not human curation.                                                                                                          |
| Academic, journalistic or other high-reliance use | Users must independently verify the particular claims and original sources they rely upon, including relevant versions, passages, counterevidence and temporal scope. Dataset labels, machine audits and earlier curation do not substitute for this use-specific inspection. This does not require reviewing unrelated records.                                                                                                                                                                 |

Use a separate audit context supplied with the bounded claim set and source references, without the proposing pass's verdicts. Combine a recorded random selection with targeted checks; set scope by risk rather than a fixed accuracy target. Check identity, passage context, locator, dates, qualifications and review labels against retrieved originals. Attribute the auditor, supplied model/version and method where known, date, sampled IDs, actual coverage, disagreements and retrieval failures. Do not claim execution independence or source access that has not been established. A bounded sample does not certify unsampled records; material errors expand checking to the affected source or relation family. Preserve supplied audits as attributed reports; machine consensus does not resolve an objection by vote.

Contested records and assessment records (`changeBasis: assessment` and `provision_assessed`) require a bounded check by a model from a different family from the one that proposed or curated them before they are merged to `main`. Record the checker, model, inputs, coverage, disagreements and retrieval failures as an attributed report, and the maintainer’s disposition of each disagreement in the repository. A same-family fresh-context check does not satisfy this requirement.

The cross-family check is also required before any admission of a research pack, whatever its records' resolution: no pack's records enter the application dataset until a model from a different family has checked them and its report is recorded. Where an adjudication by the preparing family (currently Claude) overrides the cross-family check (currently GPT) on a passage that bears an earliest-located role or a collection headline, the override must not be admitted on that adjudication alone: either send it back to the cross-family model for a short second pass, recorded with the first report, or flag it for the maintainer, whose decision is recorded before admission. (Maintainer decision of 2026-10-15, following the [policy review](policy-review-2026-10-15.md#10-cross-family-gpt-check); it writes into this policy the "before any admission" scope that research packs had adopted individually.)

### Additional-review triggers

Route these cases first to targeted source rechecking; unresolved material issues require maintainer triage:

- Machine disagreement about wording, identity, support, dates or source accessibility.
- Material quantitative claims: financial amounts, capacity, totals, bounds or comparisons that affect the reading.
- Ambiguous, conditional or redacted terms, especially legal counterparties, exclusivity and procurement commitments.
- Inferred or negative propositions, including assertions that a document omits something.
- Mutable-source problems: changed pages, missing versions, retrospective wording or an unauthenticated historical expression.
- Unusually consequential narrative placement, such as using a claim to explain founding, intellectual transmission, dependence or a governance change.
- User-reported errors or credible counterevidence.

Record the affected claim IDs, issue, evidence checked and disposition. Reserve human attention primarily for material unresolved disagreements, reported errors, consequential source ambiguities and work explicitly selected for editorial acceptance. A routine risk trigger calls for targeted machine checking, not an automatic human-review cycle. Until material issues are resolved, retain the limitation or disputed status and do not use the claim as a settled premise in a curated interpretation. A trigger is not an automatic verdict or state change. Corrections and review-state changes still require explicit authorization.

## Review states and corrections

Machine checks are not human review. Existing seed records remain exploratory records, labelled “Seed · not editorially accepted”. From 2026-10-15 the interface shows this state in plain words as “Not reviewed by an editor”, or “Not editorially accepted” where a person has checked some of the claim’s evidence (maintainer decision). This label reports stored editorial state; it does not imply that no linked evidence has been human-inspected or that every seed must enter a manual review queue. Evidence verification remains separately visible. Release suitability is assessed under the workflow above, not inferred from seed/accepted counts. `editorialState: accepted` requires an explicit human editorial decision and human-reviewed linked evidence with an identified pseudonymous reviewer. Evidence review is necessary but never sufficient for automatic acceptance. A human can accept a properly represented dispute or unresolved question without declaring the proposition true. Unadmitted machine proposals remain outside the application dataset.

`pnpm review:seed` produces the working document at `review/seed-review.md`. Each claim has empty decision and notes fields. YES, EDIT, RESEARCH MORE and REJECT are human decisions, not evidence verification values. No generator or application control imports them. After the human returns decisions, applying them requires a separate explicit task, including the scope of any evidence checks actually performed. Never infer that YES alone proves every linked passage was human-checked.

Preserve an edited working copy. Regeneration refuses to overwrite any differing existing file; use a new output path for a fresh pack. Review numbers are fixed by `review/seed-order.json`; the claim ID is the authoritative identity when exchanging decisions.

Claim IDs are stable once a record is merged to `main`. Before that, a record on an unmerged branch may be reworded in place, with the reason recorded in the repository. After merge, a correction preserves the stable claim ID when the proposition is unchanged, records the correction in the repository with its reason, and updates evidence/check dates; if the proposition changes, create a new ID and use `supersedes`. Do not erase a documented dispute or silently replace a historical framework with its current revision. Future import workflows must retain upstream version, retrieval date, licence/terms, record locator and attribution.

"Recorded in the repository" means a tracked file that a reader can find without the commit history: a note or snapshot in `review/`, a research pack's audit or decision record, or a release changelog. A commit message alone is not the record, because the public repository will start with fresh history. Commit hashes and branch names cited in tracked records refer to the private pre-release history and may not resolve publicly. (Maintainer decision of 2026-10-15, following the [policy review](policy-review-2026-10-15.md#8-publication-route-fresh-history-public-repository); the [record audit of that date](../review/repository-record-audit-2026-10-15.md) lists the gaps found.)

This change applies prospectively from 2026-09-29. It does not promote existing seeds, alter evidence verification, invent inspection or import working-pack decisions. Preserve prior decisions, audit reports and historical review packs verbatim, including earlier mandatory-review wording and uncompleted human samples. Those historical entries do not impose a new routine human-review cycle. The current dataset has no claim to completeness, earliest-use priority, or neutral sampling of the whole field.
