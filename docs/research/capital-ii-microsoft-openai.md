# Capital II dossier — Microsoft / OpenAI

Selected for the evolution of provider rights alongside investment. This is a historical term sequence through April 2026, not proof of continuing exclusivity or actual expenditure. Research checked 2026-09-25.

| Existing claims                                                | Documentary proposition and limits                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `microsoft-investment-2019`, `openai-azure-2019`               | July 2019 investment announcement and planned Azure migration form a reciprocal pair; neither traces invested dollars |
| `microsoft-investment-2023`, `openai-cloud-2023`               | January 2023 investment and exclusive-provider announcements; no amount is inferred from reporting elsewhere          |
| `openai-azure-rofr-jan-2025`, `openai-azure-api-jan-2025`      | January 2025 new-capacity right of first refusal and API exclusivity are different terms                              |
| `openai-azure-contract-oct-2025`, `openai-azure-rofr-oct-2025` | October 2025 additional $250 billion Azure contract and removal of that right; contract value is not realised spend   |

The [January 21, 2025 statement](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/01/21/microsoft-and-openai-evolve-partnership-to-drive-the-next-phase-of-ai/), partnership-terms paragraph and final paragraph, describes bidirectional revenue sharing, API exclusivity and new-capacity rights. The [October 28, 2025 statement](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/10/28/the-next-chapter-of-the-microsoft-openai-partnership/), additional-agreement terms, removes the capacity right and reports additional procurement. These mutable corporate accounts describe terms, not independent proof of payment. Stake valuation is not a new cash investment.

The [April 27, 2026 amendment statement](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/04/27/the-next-phase-of-the-microsoft-openai-partnership/), first bullet, supports two new records: `capital-ii-openai-multicloud-2026` permits all-product distribution across providers; `capital-ii-openai-azure-first-2026` records Azure-first distribution with the exact capability exception retained in the claim qualification. Primary-provider status and cross-cloud permission can coexist. Do not silently rewrite the exception's conjunction or infer cancellation of procurement commitments.

The same statement's third and fourth bullets describe ending Microsoft's revenue share to OpenAI while retaining OpenAI's payments to Microsoft through 2030 with a cap. This establishes a reported contractual revenue-sharing direction, not actual payments, their amount, or the origin of paid dollars. Keep this in prose because existing capital predicates do not precisely encode revenue sharing. No reconstructed full contract or authenticated archived expression was obtained.

The 2019 and 2023 two-edge cycles are listed with exact IDs in [the methodology](../capital-loops.md). Do not combine the 2026 permission with the 2023 investment to imply contemporaneous contract terms. Human review remains pending for both new records.
