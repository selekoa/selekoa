## CEV: recovered evidence and bounded conclusion

The [July 1, 2017 Git commit][cev-first] introduced the May entry with the current MIRI PDF as its sole citation. The [July 4 commit][cev-history] added the earlier title, August announcement and a **June 23, 2004 capture of the original HTML paper**. The retained 2019 Git expression includes that link; the observed live row cites the PDF and news archive but omits the HTML reference. This is a consequential benefit of inspecting revision history, not just following today's visible footnotes.

The [original HTML capture][cv-archive] was successfully retrieved using Wayback's `id_` replay after reader failures. The final response stayed at the requested capture; `Memento-Datetime` reported **2004-06-23 15:30:32 GMT**. The title/heading is **Collective Volition**; the byline identifies Yudkowsky and SIAI, dated May 2004. The introduction also dates its account to May. The section anchored `#cv` describes collective volition. Case-insensitive exact-name search after HTML-tag removal, entity decoding and whitespace normalization returned zero matches for **Coherent Extrapolated Volition** across the complete retrieved HTML.

The [news capture][news-archive] likewise stayed at its requested replay and reported **2006-02-20 21:14:02 GMT**. Under August 16, 2004 it announces Collective Volition and links the same original HTML path. The inspected page also returned zero exact-name matches. This is a 2006 preservation of a 2004-labelled announcement, not an August 2004 capture.

**New relative to CP II:** a retrieved period capture of the paper and a recovered institutional announcement path, independent of the later PDF's publication attribution. The June capture supplies archival evidence for contemporary **Collective Volition** wording and availability by the capture date; May remains the document's own date. It does **not** authenticate contemporary use of the exact CEV name. The current PDF still supplies only a later exact-name expression attributed to 2004. No renaming date or first exact-name use was established, and these two negative searches do not prove absence elsewhere.

The archived institution is primary evidence of its own text; Internet Archive supplies the preservation timestamp, not independent validation of the theory. This pass did not authenticate underlying WARC custody, exhaust every capture, rerun CP II's SL4 search or change its frozen findings. MediaWiki history/API remained inaccessible; Git history and the two successful raw archive replays are the bounded expansion.
