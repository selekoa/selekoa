# AI and biosecurity: candidate pack, 2026-10-28

A report on the candidate research pack in [`research/ai-bio/`](../research/ai-bio/README.md), prepared on branch `ai-bio-pack` for backlog item C of the [roadmap](../docs/roadmap.md) (AI and biosecurity, in the Frontier AI governance spine). Nothing was admitted: `data/`, the application, the schema and the releases are unchanged. The pack was machine-prepared and audited by the same model family; the cross-family check has not been run, and no record has human review.

## What the pack holds

209 candidate records, each one claim in the dataset's shape with evidence (248 quotations and 59 labelled paraphrases, all from sources recorded as primary; no record rests on a secondary source). They concern 76 document versions: 46 new candidate documents and 30 dataset documents, mostly the framework editions. 88 sources were retrieved on 2026-10-28; 27 candidate entities, mostly public bodies, publishers and seven propositions; 25 records name the dataset record they add to.

`pack.md` gives the records by strand and document, and a **timeline** of 79 dated events from October 2010 to 2026 beside the dataset's own framework and instrument dates (marked ◇).

**Hazard-safe.** Every record stays at the policy and evaluation level. 76 records carry a note saying that technical content in the source was excluded. Both auditors put the rule first and neither found a breach; between them they named eight sources holding plainly excludable content beside a cited passage, in each of which the record quotes none of it. One record keeps two register flags from the mechanical screen: the words are a benchmark's own published statement of what it measures.

## Scope choices

- **Extend, don't duplicate.** The 22 framework editions' CBRN comparison cells, the threshold and safeguard provisions already recorded for several editions, the frameworks-to-law records (Executive Order 14110's recorded sections, the AI Action Plan as a document, the Seoul Ministerial Statement's CBRN passage, the Code of Practice's systemic risks, the NIST AI 800-1 drafts, the institutes' agreements with developers) and TC260's recorded CBRN passages were not re-recorded. 25 records name the dataset record they add to.
- **Frameworks.** Biological thresholds and capability levels not yet recorded, the evaluations each framework says it runs, the safeguards it says apply at a biological threshold, and the changes between consecutive editions on these points. Scenario categories are recorded at the generality of each framework's own heading.
- **Evaluations.** One record per distinct stated result, with the report's own system version, date, setting, sample, comparison and caveats. Three results are null or limiting, and each is qualified so that a null result is not read as evidence of no uplift.
- **Arguments.** Seven candidate propositions derived from the texts retrieved, not from a list supplied in advance: current-system uplift, the physical bottleneck, the validity of published evaluations, biological design tools as a distinct case, synthesis screening as the control point, open weights, and whether assessment must cover already-skilled actors. Four carry both articulating and contesting texts.
- **Not recorded, with reasons in `pack.md`.** 47 omissions, among them: all technical content in every source (screening methodologies, in-scope criteria, threat-pathway stages, agent-naming passages); the minimum length of order subject to screening and the dates on which it changes, because the parameter is a screening criterion; OpenAI's own biological-risk publications (its site refused); bills of the 119th Congress (no full-text search was reachable); China's July 2025 global AI governance action plan (no reachable copy); the technical reports behind both joint institute summaries; and the Biological Weapons Convention's working-group sessions before the 2025 meeting.

## Checks

- **Mechanical.** All 248 quotations matched against the retrieved text of their own source, within the per-quotation and per-source limits (counting the dataset's existing quotations from the same expressions). No problems remained. The register screen over every statement, qualification, note and passage leaves one record flagged, justified in its hazard note.
- **Same-family audit.** Two fresh-context auditors checked 72 of the 207 records then merged (34 random, 38 targeted): 37 upheld, 35 corrected, none dropped, no hazard breach. See [`audit.md`](../research/ai-bio/audit.md). One finding was declined: the schema wants a verbatim quotation where the per-source budget has 22 words of headroom.
- **Widening.** Three findings were widened to their record families, as the editorial policy requires. This turned up a system-card PDF whose own footnote says a section was added after the date on its face, which neither auditor found, and a support-role defect on the proposition records that would have failed seven records at validation. Eleven unsampled records were brought onto the dataset's own precedents for provision kinds and date bases.
- **Integrator's changes before the audit.** Two candidate entities defined twice were given one definition; 25 records dated by a published card expression were moved from the snapshot time kind to an event with a publication basis, and four records resting on a page served on the check date keep the snapshot kind with no date basis, as the dataset schema has it.
- **Cross-family brief.** [`cross-family-input-2026-10-28.md`](../research/ai-bio/cross-family-input-2026-10-28.md) has 120 items: all 85 targeted records and a seeded sample of 35 of the 124 provision records. The pack's answers are in a separate verdicts file. Not run.
- `pnpm check:privacy` passed before each push. `pnpm check` was not run, because no data, schema or application file changed.

## Retrieval failures

24 in all, listed in `pack.md` and in the cross-family brief.

- **Internet Archive.** Capture pages refused connections throughout, at every stage including the audit. Every capture address in the pack is unchecked, and no record rests on one. The brief asks the checker, who can reach the Archive, to open them.
- **Three framework editions carry no record**: Meta's Advanced AI Scaling Framework v2 (every address on its host returned HTTP 400), Meta's Frontier AI Framework v1.1 (its capture reset the connection) and xAI's June 2026 framework (HTTP 404; its site refused). Nothing was recorded from memory.
- **Publishers that refused**: openai.com (403, though its CDN served the system cards), congress.gov (403), nti.org (403), one journal's reader page (a browser check), one Europe PMC service (500), and a guessed gov.cn path (404). EUR-Lex returned HTTP 202 with an empty body, as in the frameworks-to-law pack, so the AI Act came from the Publications Office repository; the UN's digital library search also returned 202, so Biological Weapons Convention documents were found by enumerating symbols.
- **No full-text bill search was reachable** (the govinfo search endpoints returned a rate limit and HTTP 405), so bills were found by testing candidate status packages and keeping those whose official title matched. Four candidates were discarded as unrelated.
- **PDF text layers.** Seven retrieved PDFs displace characters or interleave table columns. Passages from them are explicit paraphrases with locators, each saying so, and a failed search in those files is not treated as absence — one false absence was nearly recorded that way and caught in drafting.

## Open questions for the maintainer

Each has a recommended answer. The drafts hold 39 more, strand by strand; these are the ones that govern admission.

1. **The pack is 209 records against an aim of about 150.** Each of the five passes overshot its own aim, and two audit splits added two. _Recommended:_ keep all 209 for the cross-family check, which is cheaper than re-running a pass, and prune at admission if you want a smaller corpus. Each draft names the records to drop first, in order; the screening draft's list alone would take it from 47 to 37. Pruning now would discard work the checker could otherwise confirm.
2. **The proposed predicate `states_evaluation_result`** (24 records). No existing predicate carries a report's stated result with the report's own caveats; `reports_evaluation_of` names a category and nothing more. _Recommended:_ add it with an `attributes.evaluation` block of four fields (what was measured, by what kind of method, the result in the report's words, the caveats the report gives), and a validation rule that a record of it is dated by its report and carries the report's caveats. Without it, the evaluations strand cannot be admitted.
3. **`evaluation_engagement` admits only organisation subjects**, and the UK AI Security Institute is typed `public_body` in the dataset, so two records cannot be admitted as structured. The dataset is already inconsistent: the US institute is typed `organisation`. _Recommended:_ widen the predicate's subjects to include `public_body`, rather than retyping either institute.
4. **The provision kinds the schema lacks.** The pack needs kinds for a stated date of effect, for an instrument's standing (voluntary or binding) and for its addressees. _Recommended:_ keep the pack as it now stands, which uses the dataset's own precedents (`threshold` for a stated date, as `omni-entry-into-force` and `ny-effective-date` do; `threshold` for what an instrument covers; `required_action` for standing) and says so in each qualification. If you would rather be exact, add `date` and `standing` kinds in the same schema change as question 2.
5. **Proposition codes.** The pack's B1–B7 clash with the dataset's existing B1–B6, and validation rejects duplicate codes. _Recommended:_ re-letter the whole set once at admission to a free letter (D is unused; the dataset uses A, B, C, E, G, M and Y), and rename the records then. One identifier (`ab-arg-b2-batalis-articulates`) also names a stance the audit took away from it and should be renamed in the same pass.
6. **One proposition now has no articulating text.** After the audit downgraded its one articulation to a context record, the physical-bottleneck proposition carries three contesting texts and one context record. _Recommended:_ admit it as it stands — the schema allows an unheld earliest-located role — and record the gap as a search result. A later pass should search the paywalled venues the arguments pass could not reach before anyone reads the imbalance as a finding.
7. **The quotation budget on two framework editions.** `source-fgf-2026` has one word of headroom and `source-ms-fgf-2026` four, after the dataset's existing quotations, and `source-rsp-3-2` has 22. Four records rest on paraphrase for that reason alone, one of them where the schema wants a quotation. _Recommended:_ accept the paraphrases, except for the declined audit finding, where an elided quotation of the sentence's outer limbs fits in 16 counted words. Retiring an existing dataset quotation is the alternative and is your decision.
8. **Undated and mutable developer documents.** Three sources state no date or only a month, and four state a later update than the date on their face. _Recommended:_ admit them as the pack now treats them (the stated date where there is one, the unknown time kind where there is none, a snapshot for a page that states nothing, and the expression checked recorded in every case), and ask the cross-family checker to compare the released and updated expressions of the two institute summary pages against Archive captures, since neither page says what changed.
9. **Biological Weapons Convention national statements.** Three records treat a state party's working paper as a document stating provisions, because the publication predicates take an organisation or public body as subject and this pack creates no country entities. _Recommended:_ admit them as provision records now, and revisit when the country entity type added in schema 2.19 is used more widely; a `published` record with a country subject would be the better shape.
10. **The 24 October 2024 national security memorandum's status.** No revocation was retrieved, and Executive Order 14179's review of actions taken under Executive Order 14110 does not state one. _Recommended:_ record its provisions as stated and dated, with no status beyond issuance, as the pack does. Do not infer a revocation. A short follow-up search of 2025–2026 presidential documents would settle it.
11. **The NIST centre's name.** The page retrieved on 2026-10-28 names a Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI), reached by redirect from the Center for AI Standards and Innovation's address. _Recommended:_ keep the identifiers separate until a naming announcement is retrieved, and then record the change as a dated name on one entity, as the dataset does for the US institute's earlier renaming (its `datedNames` carry "U.S. AI Safety Institute" until June 2025 and the Center for AI Standards and Innovation after it), in a separate task. This is the same renaming pattern the frameworks-to-law pack met in the "SI" wording caveat.
12. **Admission order.** _Recommended:_
    1. Run the cross-family brief, and have the checker open the archive copies.
    2. Decide questions 2–5, which govern the evaluations strand (24 records), two engagement records, the provision kinds and every proposition record.
    3. Admit in two parts: the frameworks, screening and government-measures records first, which need only the provision-kind decision, then the evaluations and arguments records, which need the new predicate and the re-lettering.
13. **The three unread framework editions.** _Recommended:_ ask the cross-family checker to open their Archive captures and report their biological provisions, and do not admit the frameworks strand as covering 22 editions while three are unread. The pack says 19 throughout.
