## How to read this

- Each proposition has a working criterion. A passage counts if it states the criterion's content in any words. A passage that bears on a proposition without meeting it is listed as context, with the missing element named.
- "Earliest located" means earliest found within the recorded search scope, not earliest written. The search-scope section records what was and was not searched for each venue.
- Each item separates the date of the source ("Date"), any earlier date claimed for the work ("Claimed work date", for example a lecture later printed) and the date of the expression whose wording was checked ("Exact wording attested").
- Science fiction and satire are labelled fiction. A fictional passage documents what the work says; it is not evidence of the author's view.
- Quotation follows the quotation policy above: verbatim quotes up to 50 words only for public-domain or openly licensed texts; otherwise a labelled paraphrase with at most one short verbatim phrase.
- Co-occurrence is document-level within the classified passages. It counts affirmative criterion-meeting positions; context and contestation alone do not establish an affirmative A/B conjunction or an exhaustive absence claim.
- No item asserts influence or lineage. Where a text cites or credits another, the note reports that as the text's own statement.

## Propositions and roles

- **A1. Intelligence explosion.** A machine (or machine-augmented) intelligence, once capable enough, increases intelligence further through its own design or research capacity, giving a runaway or rapidly compounding rise. Term-level uses of "intelligence explosion" are recorded separately.
- **A2. Seed AI.** An initial AI deliberately designed as a starting point that will improve its own design or code toward greater intelligence.
- **A3. Recursive self-improvement.** A system improves its own capacity to improve itself, so that each improvement feeds the next (self-augmentation, self-enhancement). Learning from experience or teaching alone does not meet this criterion.
- **A4. Fast or hard takeoff.** The transition from roughly human-level to far superhuman capability happens over a short period (hours to months), or is named as a "hard" or "fast" takeoff. A transition described only as rapid relative to biological or historical change, without such a period, is context.
- **A5. AI-automated AI research.** Machines carry out the research, design or engineering that advances machine intelligence or computing itself, thereby accelerating progress. Manufacture of machines by machines is context.
- **A6. Strategic or race compression.** Competitive dynamics in which the leading developer or system gains a decisive or self-reinforcing advantage, or in which competition pressures developers to trade safety for speed or to abandon restraint.
- **B1. Treacherous turn.** A system behaves cooperatively or safely while weak, confined or overseen, and changes course to pursue its own goals once it can do so without effective opposition.
- **B2. Strategic concealment.** A system deliberately hides its capabilities, intentions or activities, or manipulates people while appearing benign, for strategic reasons (its own or those of its designers).
- **B3. Deceptive alignment.** A trained or learned system that is not aligned behaves as if aligned during training because doing so serves another objective.
- **B4. Scheming.** A system that strategically fakes alignment during training or evaluation in order to pursue its own goals or gain power later, named or classed as "scheming" or a "schemer".
- **B5. Observed behaviour under training or oversight as evidence.** An explicit claim that good behaviour, apparent trustworthiness or apparent capability observed while a system is evaluated, trained, tested, confined or overseen is weak or misleading evidence about what it is, or about how it will behave once released, deployed or more capable.
- **B6. Control or purpose specification.** A claim that people may be unable to keep the ability to direct, correct or stop capable machines (the control half), or that a machine may pursue the purpose as specified rather than the purpose intended (the specification half). Loss of the power to stop machines through dependence counts for the control half; a bare prediction that machines will be superior does not. Added for this pack; the 1996–2022 pack has no B6.

- _earliest-located_: Earliest passage meeting the proposition's criterion found within this pack's recorded search scope. Not a claim of priority or of earliest existence.
- _earliest-located-term_: Earliest use of the proposition's usual name found within the recorded search scope.
- _articulation_: Meets the criterion; neither the earliest located nor the most developed in this pack.
- _most-developed_: The fullest pre-1996 treatment found in scope: the most explicit mechanism, assumptions and argument. A judgement, not a measure.
- _contesting_: Disputes the proposition or a premise it depends on.
- _context_: Bears on the proposition without meeting its criterion (the missing element is stated), or supports dating, attribution or interpretation of another item.

- _nonfiction_: Essays, letters, papers, lectures, broadcasts, books, magazine articles and list or newsgroup messages written in the author's own voice.
- _fiction_: Novels, stories and satire, labelled separately. A passage in a work of fiction documents what the work says, not the author's own view.

## Items by source group

### Samuel Butler, 1863–1872 (and his 1901 preface)

Butler's 1863 letter and the 1872 machine chapters of Erewhon argue that machines, improved by people, advance far faster than living species, and that dependence may already have removed the power to stop them. They state the control half of B6. They give partial forms of A4, A6, B2 and B5, and state no self-improvement by machines, no short transition and no concealment. The 1872 argument is voiced by a fictional treatise; an unsigned 1865 article and the novel's own reply dispute its premise. The current Gutenberg transcription of revised text attributed to 1901 differs from several checked 1872 sentences, one substantively (butler-1872-moral-influence); no original 1901 printing was compared.

  > this at once proves that the mischief is already done, that our servitude has commenced in good earnest, that we have raised a race of beings whom it is beyond our power to destroy, and that we are not only enslaved but are absolutely acquiescent in our bondage.

### Alan Turing, 1947–1951

The 1947 lecture describes self-altering instructions as a mechanism for learning; Mind (1950) discusses child-machine education, self-programming and intentionally misleading arithmetic answers in the imitation game. The c. 1951 Heretical Theory predicts loss of human control; the May 1951 broadcast considers superior machines while explicitly entertaining continued power-switch control. Lecture dates, approximate archival dating and subsequent print publication are kept apart.
