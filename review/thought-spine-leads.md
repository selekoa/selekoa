## 2. Better copies of texts already recorded

These would change dates or wording, not classifications, unless the earlier version differs.

- **Turing.** King's College archive AMT/B/4 (Intelligent Machinery, a Heretical Theory: typescript and carbon), AMT/B/20, AMT/B/5 (the 1951 broadcast script) and AMT/B/1 (the 1947 lecture). Compare the variants and establish the lecture dates; no audio recording or delivery transcript was found. The 1948 National Physical Laboratory report _Intelligent Machinery_ is not among the records admitted.
- **Butler.** “The Mechanical Creation” (_The Reasoner_, London, 1865-07-01), which Butler's 1901 preface names as Butler's own rewrite; the 1865 “Lucubratio Ebria” column images (the captures returned HTTP 403, so its wording is uncorrected OCR); a printed 1901 _Erewhon_ to replace the Gutenberg transcription; the 1872 second edition.
- **Good 1965.** The printed volume's imprint (Open Library gives 1965, Crossref 1966). Good's chapter p. 35 discusses a chess win produced by a stored opening trap, a possible C1 record not yet classified.
- **Vinge.** The complete _Omni_ issue of January 1983 (masthead and page); the delivered VISION-21 version of March 1993 and the _Whole Earth Review_ variant of Winter 1993.
- **Drexler.** The 1986 printing of _Engines of Creation_. Its p. 70 (web edition) describes technical AI and a “sudden, massive lead”, a possible A6 record whose wording is attested only from 1997.
- **Moravec.** _Mind Children_ (1988) original pages; the 1976 text of “Today's Computers, Intelligent Machines and Our Future” (the author-hosted file is marked “this version 1978”).
- **Mid-century texts.** Minsky 1961, Michie 1968 (_Theoria to Theory_ 2) and Solomonoff 1985 (_Human Systems Management_ 5) in their original journal pages; Samuel's 1960 reply in _Science_ 132, pp. 741–742 (the publisher and JSTOR refused retrieval), which would settle who wrote the indexed abstract; Wiener's 1959 AAAS lecture.
- **Extropy.** Original scans of issues 1–6; issue 6 survives only as a later electronic edition. The Mondo 2000 interview with Vinge (Fall 1989) cited there.
- **1996–2008 web texts.** Yudkowsky's pages of 1996–1999 (no capture holds their text); earlier versions of Creating Friendly AI; the printed 2008 chapter on AI and global risk; the conference versions of Shulman 2009 and of LOGI 2007, whose MIRI reprints are marked “minor changes”; the 2008-12-10 or 2008-12-11 date of Hanson's “What Core Argument?”.
- **Arbital.** The original arbital.com pages or captures, to date “Context disaster”, “Programmer deception” and “Optimization daemons”; the viewer's timestamps may be last-edit times.
- **Usenet.** Original transport headers for Dani Eder's sci.nanotech post of 1994-05-23 (known from a 1997 relay); the Rutgers sci.nanotech singularity discussion archive linked in Sandberg's 1997 index.

## 3. Searches not run, or blocked

- **Extropians list, 1991 to 1996-07-25.** Not in the public archive used. Subscriber exports, if legally available, need header and publication-status checks. Every 1996–1998 headline is exposed to this gap.
- **HathiTrust and Google Books.** Both refused scripted requests on 2026-10-06 (HTTP 403 and 429). Phrase searches for “intelligence explosion”, “ultraintelligent”, “machines take control”, “self-improving machine” and the B6 control wording before 1965, and before 1863, should be run from an interactive session or library access.
- **The pre-1863 screen** used Butler's own wording. Earlier statements of the control idea in other words (for example in 1830s–1850s writing on machinery) were not sought.
- **Oxford English Dictionary.** Its citations for “intelligence explosion”, “singularity” (technological sense), “seed AI” and “takeoff” were not checked. The OED is a competent authority: if it dates a first use, the collection should attribute that statement to it and keep it apart from the earliest-located record.
- **Usenet.** Google Groups refused requests (HTTP 429); only ten Carnegie Mellon bundles of comp.ai and comp.ai.philosophy were searched.
- **LessWrong and the Alignment Forum.** No full-text search; comments and linked posts were not read.
- **Christiano's ai-alignment.com posts (2016–2018)**, which could hold an earlier B3 passage than 2018-05-05.
- **Lenat's Eurisko papers (1983)**, Ashby and von Neumann, as A3 and A5 leads among mid-century writers.
- **Fiction.** Not surveyed. Good's chapter says the control point is made seldom outside science fiction. Candidates include E. M. Forster, “The Machine Stops” (1909), Karel Čapek, _R.U.R._ (1920) and Jack Williamson, “With Folded Hands” (1947); any admission would be labelled fiction.
- **B6 and C1–C3 after mid-1996.** The 1996–2022 search did not look for them, so no most-developed text is named. Specification gaming, corrigibility and the Turing-test literature after 1996 are the obvious places to start.

### New earlier lead: Weizenbaum 1966 (C1)

Joseph Weizenbaum, “ELIZA — a computer program for the study of natural language communication between man and machine”, _Communications of the ACM_ 9(1), January 1966, pp. 36–45 (Crossref metadata; the transcription's “36–35” is a typo). The check read the Discussion section in a later [course-site transcription](https://courses.cs.umbc.edu/331/papers/eliza.html): it distinguishes the speaker's sense of being understood from knowledge, insight or reasoning attributed to the program, which would meet C1. The publisher PDF returned HTTP 403.

Before admission: obtain a lawful original CACM scan; compare the Discussion paragraph and give its printed page and paragraph; add the paper to the search note; admit its criterion-matching passage as a separate record; then recompute the C1 headline by attested wording. Michie 1968 stays as a later articulation. No first-appearance claim.
