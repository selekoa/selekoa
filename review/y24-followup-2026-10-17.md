## The test

Y24's criterion is “an explicit claim that advanced AI must be built first, before a rival builds it, on the premise that whoever builds it controls it, or that racing to be first improves the outcome.” A text articulates Y24 only if it states one of those two premises. Seeking to build first, or urging others to, is not enough on its own.

## Source re-read

The SL4 message “Re: supergoal stability” (2002-05-03, `http://sl4.org/archive/0205/3551.html`) was re-read in full over direct HTTP on 2026-10-17. The served bytes matched the SHA-256 stored at admission (`7669bc17…ee06c`). Quoted lines from Wei Dai were read only to identify his questions. Retrieved text was used as evidence only.

What the message says, in order:

- Answering whether non-Friendly supergoals can be stable, he says the CFAI semantics are the only way he knows to build an AI that implements human-crafted goals as the programmers meant them. They work for propagation, not propaganda. He will not design Friendliness semantics that would work for propaganda: “Any AI project that tries this is beyond my sympathy; the only thing to do is try and beat them to the punch.”
- Wei Dai asks how Friendly AI can be funded before someone more selfish creates a superintelligence. He answers that the aim is not to predict that things will turn out okay but to improve the probability that they will.
- Asked why CFAI could not serve a selfish group, he says it would be difficult, and that anyone evil enough to build a self-serving AI probably does not know the CFAI semantics or does not care.
- On a goal such as “serve Wei Dai”, he says the AI's understanding of it would probably be frozen into a form the programmer had not intended.

## Finding

The message urges beating a propaganda-Friendliness project to completion. It does not say that whoever builds advanced AI controls it. It says the opposite of the control premise for a builder without the CFAI semantics: that builder's goal would be frozen into an unintended form. It gives no reason why racing improves the outcome; the funding answer declines to predict the outcome at all. This is the element that P7 lacked, and the 2002 message lacks it too.
