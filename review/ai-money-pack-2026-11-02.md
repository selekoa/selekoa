# AI and money: candidate pack, 2026-11-02

The candidate pack [`research/ai-money/`](../research/ai-money/README.md) on branch `ai-money-pack`, from `main` at v0.26.1. It records what monetary and financial authorities have said about machine intelligence. It changes no data, code, schema or release. Prepared by Claude (four drafting passes by source group, integration and a fresh-context same-family audit); the cross-family check (GPT, via Codex) has not run. Nothing is admitted.

## Scope

The roadmap entry "AI and money" (maintainer order of 2026-11-02, "Most interesting", item 2; recorded on the unmerged branch `roadmap-next-collections`) and the task prompt of the same date. Full scope: [scope.md](../research/ai-money/scope.md).

- **Bodies:** the BIS (Annual Economic Reports, working papers, FSI Insights, IFC, Innovation Hub), the Basel Committee, the FSB, the IMF, the ESRB, the EBA, EIOPA; the ECB and ECB Banking Supervision, the Bank of England (with the PRA and FCA), the Bank of Finland, the Bundesbank, the Riksbank; the Federal Reserve Board, the PBoC, the Bank of Japan, MAS, HKMA and the RBI.
- **Twelve propositions (U01–U12)** in four groups: financial stability (correlated behaviour, provider concentration, cyber and fraud, opacity), monetary policy (nowcasting, AI's effects mattering for policy), payments (AI agents and payment flows, AI agents and tokenised money), supervision and central banks' own work (suptech, firms' risk management, central banks' own capacity, whether existing rules cover AI).
- **Voice.** Every document is an institutional text, an official's speech or remarks, a staff paper or (added at integration) a committee report. A speech is the speaker's, with its disclaimer, never the institution's policy.
- **Institutional events** that an authority's own text states, with stage (announced or stated done).
- **Earliest located** per proposition is decided by the date of the wording across all voices; the earliest located institutional text is shown beside it as derived information.

## Open questions, with recommendations

1. **Committee voice.** Adopted at integration for the RBI FREE-AI Committee report and the ESRB Advisory Scientific Committee report. _Recommended:_ keep it; a committee report is attributed to the committee, never to the authority.
2. **IMF GFSR chapter voice.** Its disclaimer attributes the views to IMF staff, but no chapter authors are printed in the copy read. _Recommended:_ keep institution voice with the disclaimer shown, and switch to staff if the PDF (Codex list, item 6) prints authors. Apply the same rule to the ECB FSR special feature (recorded as staff, with named authors).
3. **Earliest located across voices.** The headline goes to a speech or staff paper on U01, U05, U06, U07, U10, U11 and U12. _Recommended:_ keep one headline by wording date across voices, and show the earliest located institutional text beside it, as derived information (as the pack does), rather than a tier rule like the economics collection's.
4. **Phillips 1997 on U10.** AI is one item in a list of underwriting tools. _Recommended:_ keep it as earliest located with its qualification; the cross-family check should confirm it meets the criterion.
5. **Carney 2018 on U06.** He places AI within a "Fourth Industrial Revolution". _Recommended:_ keep as earliest located if the cross-family check agrees; otherwise Cook (22 September 2023) holds it.
6. **Relays.** Relays (the IFC Bulletin 44 overview of a Central Bank of Aruba presentation on U05; the ECB innovation platform outcome report on U07; FS2/23 respondents on U12) are not eligible for earliest located. _Recommended:_ keep the rule; retrieve and record the Aruba paper in its own right if it is a central bank's text.
7. **U08 has no articulation.** Four texts bear on it (BIS AER 2025 and 2026, Ueda 2026, the ECB call of 28 September 2026). The only passage close to the criterion is in MAS-convened industry white papers (MindForge 2024, SAFR 2026), which MAS labels industry-led. _Recommended:_ keep U08 as a proposition with context records only, shown as "no located text meets the criterion", and keep the industry white papers out of scope.
8. **Candidate predicates for events.** Six events (a concluded project, a proof of concept, projects stated as existing, a standing priority, a programme stated as developed) fit none of `unit_of`, `launched`, `published` or `announced_plan`. _Recommended:_ at admission record them as `status_stated` snapshots where that predicate fits, or hold them; no new predicate for this pack alone.
9. **AER 2024 date.** The chapter page prints 30 June 2024; the BIS press release gives a release on 25 June. _Recommended:_ keep the printed date; no headline depends on it.
10. **Securities regulators.** The SEC (Bauguess, 21 June 2017, on AI and big data in supervision) may be earlier than the U09 holder; sec.gov refused this environment. IOSCO's 2021 report is on the Codex list. _Recommended:_ include securities regulators only through IOSCO and the authorities already in scope, and record the SEC speech only if the maintainer widens the scope.
11. **Leads not recorded.** Hernández de Cos, April 2024 (full text not reached); the BIS speech of 8 October 2025 on AI and central banks; the Federal Reserve Board's Payments Innovation Roundtable summary of 26 May 2026 (uses "agentic commerce"; largely a relay); the Bundesbank and BaFin papers on machine learning in risk models (2021–22); the Riksbank's AI Sweden partnership; DNB's 2019 principles and the ACPR's 2020 discussion document (both on the Codex list); Norges Bank and Danmarks Nationalbank (not searched). _Recommended:_ one bounded follow-up pass after the Codex retrieval, before admission.

## How this becomes a collection

1. **Cross-family check** on `codex/ai-money-check`, blind first, with the Codex retrieval list.
2. **Adjudication and maintainer decisions** on the open questions above and on each disagreement, recorded in this note under "Maintainer decisions".
3. **Admission** on a data branch from `main`, in two parts by the size rule. Documents become dataset entities (organisations for authorities not yet in the dataset; persons for speakers, without characterisation); records use `articulates_proposition`, `contests_proposition` and `bears_on_proposition`; events use existing predicates or are held. The voice and disclaimer need a home: either `attributes.thought` gains `voice` and the source a `disclaimer` passage (a schema change, so a minor release after the records on existing fields), or voice is carried in the statement and the disclaimer as a context evidence item (no schema change). _Recommended:_ the second for the first part, the schema change only if the interface needs to filter by voice.
4. **Collection definition** in `src/reading/` on the model of the economics collection, with a "How we searched" note from the search log and retrieval failures, and a reader-facing title such as "What central banks and financial authorities have said about AI". Version: minor.

### The pattern in the earliest-located answers

Six of the twelve blind searches are material. On four propositions the checker located a text earlier than the pack's (U05, U06, U09, and U12's institutional comparator); on two more the earliest-located role turns on how the criterion is read (U07, U08). Every earlier text lies either before 2016, where the pack's systematic searches start (Haldane 2015, the Czech National Bank 2005, a Bank of Canada lead of 1999), or in a body the pack did not search or could not reach (the Czech National Bank, the SEC, the Bank of Canada; DNB refused this environment). The pack's earliest-located headlines are therefore the products of its 2016 start date and its list of bodies; they are weakest where the located date sits close to 2016. This is not corrected by the adjudication. It is the reason for decision 11 on the decision sheet (one bounded pass before 2016 over the bodies in scope, before admission), and the "How we searched" note must state both limits.

A second pattern: three of the eleven material findings are on U12 (A2.30, A2.46, B52), in both directions. The pack read a technology-neutral approach as coverage by existing rules (FSB 2026), read a call for guidance in specific areas as a contest (FSI Insights 63), and strengthened a modal statement (ESRB ASC). The other U12 contesting records (IMF 2021, RBI FREE-AI Committee, Breeden 2026) were checked only in part (Breeden not at all) and should be re-read with the same rule before admission: a call for guidance or adaptation in specific areas is not, alone, a contest of broad coverage.

### Disagreements and boundary questions for the maintainer

1. **U07: Waller, 29 September 2025 (A1.7, A2.42; sheet decisions 15 and 6).** Held under the override rule. The checker reads bears on: agents "executing tasks on behalf of a person or company" in a payments-efficiency passage do not state that agents initiate, negotiate or execute payments, and the next sentence is about compliance. The pack reads articulates: the tasks are named as a way AI agents improve payments. On rereading here, the checker's reading fits the criterion's words ("payments and transactions") better. If Waller becomes context, the headline goes to the ECB outcome report (September 2025, a relay of workshop participants) if relays are eligible, otherwise BIS Working Paper 1310 (10 November 2025, staff). The checker notes that the relay exclusion (open question 6) was not stated in the brief.
2. **U08: BIS Annual Economic Report 2025, chapter III (A1.8; sheet decision 7).** Held. The checker reads the passage on AI agents embedding pre-screening checks in payment instructions on a tokenised correspondent-banking platform as articulating U08's second limb ("AI would interact with such forms of money in ways that matter for the monetary system"); it would become U08's earliest located text. The pack reads bears on: the agents do compliance reporting (Box B calls them copilots for suspicious-activity reports), and the text does not say that this matters for the monetary system. The pack's missing element had addressed only the first limb; it now addresses both.
3. **U05: Czech National Bank Working Paper 11/2005 (A1.5; sheet decision 16).** Held on scope. The official abstract (Hlaváček, Koňák and Čada, December 2005, staff) reports that a neural-network model forecasts currency in circulation better, for liquidity forecasting; the paper itself was not read. It meets the criterion on the abstract, but the Czech National Bank is not among the bodies the scope names. A Bank of Canada working paper of January 1999 is a lead only (empty HTTP 202 responses).
4. **U09: Bauguess (SEC), 21 October 2016 (A1.9; sheet decision 10).** Held on scope (open question 10). The checker read the speech: machine learning for market-risk assessment and to direct examination and investigative staff; the speaker's own views. His speech of 21 June 2017 also precedes the FSB report of 27 June 2017. The FSB report would stay the earliest located institutional text and the earliest located use of "SupTech" either way.

None of the four is overruled by the preparer.
