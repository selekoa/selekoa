## Scope

A dated record of predictions about machine intelligence: who predicted what, when, by what date or within what horizon, with what stated probability, in what form. [scope.md](../research/predictions/scope.md) was written before research. Records cover 1950–2026 in six groups: early statements (Turing, the Dartmouth proposal, Simon and Newell, Good, Michie's poll, Lighthill's report of correspondents), essays and books 1980–2008 (de Garis, Vinge, Yudkowsky, Bostrom, Moravec, Kurzweil, Legg), expert surveys (Baum et al., Müller and Bostrom, Grace et al. 2017, Zhang et al., the 2022 ESPAI, Grace et al. 2024), the Kapor–Kurzweil Long Bet, researchers' forecasts 2018–2024 (Brooks, Karnofsky, Carlsmith, Cotra, Aschenbrenner, Bengio et al.) and laboratories and their leaders 2023–2026 (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Altman, Amodei, AI 2027). Counts are in [pack.md](../research/predictions/pack.md).

Texts the dataset already holds were used first: 16 of the pack’s 41 sources carry a dataset source ID. Where a source could not be retrieved here, its quotation was matched against the dataset's checked passage and labelled so. Three records quote sources this environment cannot reach (Simon and Newell 1958; two openai.com pages); they are held as "awaiting retrieval" and listed for Codex with 12 further items, among them Metaculus captures, Kurzweil's books, Minsky 1967 and 1970, Legg's blog posts and the Sandberg–Bostrom survey ([codex-retrieval.md](../research/predictions/codex-retrieval.md)).

No platform figure is recorded yet: Metaculus pages must be read as dated archive captures, which only Codex can retrieve.

## Rules applied

- **No judgement of fulfilment.** No record says whether a prediction came true, was early, late, right or wrong. There is no outcome field. The build rejects fulfilment and priority words outside quotation marks.
- **Words kept.** The predicted event, the horizon's words and the probability's words must each lie inside a quoted passage of the record (checked by the build). Hedges ("I believe", "may", "could", "plausible", "it is possible") and conditions are recorded with the prediction.
- **Horizons as stated.** Six kinds: absolute (a printed year), period ("the 2020s", "within the twentieth century", "late 2026 or early 2027"), relative ("within ten years", "in a few thousand days"), quantile (a survey's or person's probability levels), vague ("within upcoming decades") and figure (Michie's poll, whose horizon exists only in a chart). Calendar years are stored only where the source prints them. "In about fifty years' time" (Turing, 1950) is not stored as 2000.
- **Surveys.** The figure belongs to the survey report, with its respondents, date and statistic as the report states them; no respondent is named.
- **Bets.** Each party and position as the bet page states it; stakes as stated.
- **Reported statements.** Lighthill's unnamed correspondents are recorded as his report, in his words.
- **Retrospective reports.** Cotra's 2020 estimate appears only as her 2022 report of it, flagged as retrospective.

## Data model proposal

Recommended: **one new predicate, `states_prediction`, from a document to a literal**, with an `attributes.prediction` block. The subject is the text, as with proposition records (`articulates_proposition`): a record says what a document states, dated by the document. The person is reached through the existing `authored` claims; a survey through its report; a bet through its page; a platform figure through a capture document of a given date.

| Field                                  | Content                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `form`                                 | `stated-forecast`, `public-bet`, `expert-survey`, `platform-question`, `reported-statement`                                                                                                                                                  |
| `predictor`                            | Entity IDs and a display label; kind person, persons, organisation, survey or platform                                                                                                                                                       |
| `event`                                | The predicted event as quoted (must lie within the claim's evidence quotation), or a labelled paraphrase, with an optional note                                                                                                              |
| `horizon`                              | `asStated` (the words), `kind` (absolute, period, relative, quantile, vague, figure), `years` (only printed years), `interval` (amount, unit, from what), `relation` (by, before, around, no earlier than, as early as, window), `precision` |
| `probability`                          | As worded, or absent                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `conditions`, `hedge`                  | As worded                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `survey`                               | Respondents, date conducted, statistic, as the report states them                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `bet`                                  | Platform, parties with entity IDs and positions, stakes as stated                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `capture`                              | For a platform figure: the capture date and address                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `attested`, `claimed`, `retrospective` | As in schema 2.11 and 2.12: a later copy of the wording, an earlier claimed date that does not date the wording, an earlier estimate reported later                                                                                          |

The claim is `explicit` and `supported`: supported means that the document states the prediction, never that the prediction is true or came true. The claim's `time` is the source date (an event), never the target date; the target lives only in `horizon`. Validation would enforce: years only for absolute and quantile horizons and only years that appear in `asStated`; the event inside the evidence quotation; no `posed` derivation; no `contested` resolution (a later dispute about a prediction is a separate record, not a contest of the statement).

Rejected alternative: extending `attributes.thought.estimates` (schema 2.13). It needs a proposition, lacks form, horizon kind and bet or survey structure, and would tie predictions to collections of arguments.

The pack's record shape is this proposal, so an admission maps field by field. New entities needed at admission include persons not yet in the dataset (among them Ray Kurzweil, Mitchell Kapor, Rodney Brooks, Joseph Carlsmith, Hugo de Garis, James Lighthill, Herbert Simon, Allen Newell, Seth Baum, Vincent C. Müller) and about 30 document and source records. Per the operating guide this is a schema change, so admission is a minor release; no part of the pack fits an existing predicate.

## Open questions, with recommendations

1. **Data model.** Adopt `states_prediction` with the attribute block above, the document as subject. _Recommendation: yes._
2. **"Events on record near the target date."** The prompt asks whether a later section may list dataset records of the predicted event near the target date. A scan of the dataset for the events' own words (for example "world chess champion", "Turing test", "join the workforce", "country of geniuses", "superintelligence") found no dated record of any predicted event: every match is another text that discusses the term. Building the section would therefore mean choosing records that "match" a prediction, and that choice is itself a judgement of fulfilment. _Recommendation: do not build it._ Instead, the prediction page may link a printed target year to the dataset's existing browse-by-year page for that year, as a neutral context link with the label "What the dataset records for 2029", never placed beside the prediction as an outcome. If the maintainer prefers the section, restrict it to records whose statement repeats the predicted event's key phrase verbatim, show it collapsed under "Events on record near the target date (no judgement of fulfilment is made)", and have the cross-family check confirm each pairing.
3. **Relative horizons on a timeline.** "Within ten years" could be plotted as source date plus ten years. _Recommendation: do not compute it in data;_ a page may draw the interval as a bracket from the source date, labelled with the source's words, but never a point at a computed year. Period and vague horizons are listed, not plotted.
4. **Held records.** Simon and Newell (1958) and the two OpenAI pages of 2023 rest on unverified wording. _Recommendation: hold until Codex confirms; admit only then._
5. **Reported statements.** Lighthill's report of correspondents is admissible as his report. _Recommendation: admit with the form label; add the Life (1970) report of Minsky the same way if Codex finds it, together with any later statement by Minsky about the quotation._
6. **Dates as section headings.** Moravec's "The Age of Robots" (1993) dates sections ("The Medium Run (around 2050)") without dating sentences. _Recommendation: not recorded;_ a heading frames a scenario but is not a sentence that states an event by a date.
7. **Live pages.** Bostrom (1998), Kurzweil (2001), Brooks (2018), the 2022 ESPAI page and AI 2027 were read as live pages. _Recommendation: admit with the wording attested from the retrieval date,_ and ask Codex for captures near publication (two are in the list).
8. **Collection and page.** _Recommendation:_ a collection "Predictions on record" under the hub "Human thought and institutions around machine intelligence", with one page:
   - a chart with the source date on the horizontal axis and the printed target year on the vertical axis, for absolute and quantile horizons only, one mark per year a record prints (a survey's quantiles joined by a thin line), shapes by form;
   - beside it, the same records as a keyboard-accessible table (predictor, source date, event in quotation marks, horizon as stated, probability, form), sortable by source date or target, with relative, period, vague and figure horizons in the table only;
   - filters by form and by decade of the source date; each row opens the record with its passage, locator and source;
   - no "today" line, no diagonal or shading that marks past target dates, no colour for "passed", and no count of predictions by outcome;
   - an export as CSV with the same columns.
     The page's note says what the collection does not do: judge fulfilment, rank predictors, or combine estimates.
9. **Retrospective estimate.** Cotra's 2020 estimate is recorded only from her 2022 report of it. _Recommendation: keep it flagged as retrospective; replace or pair it with a record from the 2020 report when that is read._
10. **Later additions.** Metaculus figures, Kurzweil's 1999 and 2005 dates, Legg's blog distributions, Simon (1965), Minsky (1967) and the Sandberg–Bostrom survey wait on Codex retrieval. _Recommendation: one Codex batch with the cross-family check, then a small supplementary pass before admission._

### The data model: answers to the checker's questions

The checker's conclusion: adopt the document-to-literal approach, not unchanged. Agreed. Against the proposed `states_prediction`:

- **Fit as proposed:** 30 (the `bet` block already holds parties and positions; the predictor is not derived from authorship), 6 (`attested` for the 1986 reprint, the 1972 poll in the date note) and 36 (`retrospective`).
- **Do not fit unchanged:** 12 (the event is a labelled paraphrase, but the proposal's validation sentence requires the event inside the quotation; the field table allows a paraphrase), 45 ("should be able to" is a forecast or an aim), 17, 35 and 37 (several probability–horizon points, or two estimate versions, in one free-text block), 7 and 8 (unnamed reported speakers; `authored` would give Lighthill), 4 (the horizon's anchor is the 1957 address, not the 1958 document date), and the proposed ban on `contested` and `unresolved`, which would also forbid documentary disputes over wording, attribution or version.
- **Smallest change:** (1) make the paraphrase route explicit (`event.type: quote | paraphrase`; a paraphrase requires a note, and the horizon words must still lie in a quotation); (2) an optional `mode: forecast | forecast-or-aim`, default forecast; (3) an optional `points` list, each item a probability as worded with its horizon words and, where needed, its estimate version and date, required when a record has more than one; (4) predictor kind `unnamed` with a `reporter` entity for reported statements; (5) an optional `occasion` (date and kind: address, poll, estimate) that `interval.from` may name; (6) allow `contested` and `unresolved` for documentary disputes only, keep "supported" meaning only that the document states the prediction, and refuse admission of any record whose quotation was not checked against a copy. No outcome field in any case.
