# Selekoa product review

Model: Codex, based on GPT-6. The precise serving-model variant is not exposed in this session.

Date: 2026-10-04. Reviewed `origin/main` at `408a915ea78baf5da896cc9e94281b4a3c13e9b5` in an isolated worktree. Only this report is changed.

## Judgement

Selekoa has reached a usable documentary research prototype. It has not demonstrated the historical depth, researcher time savings or durable source preservation needed to justify its larger ambition. The next useful milestone is a small, reproducible governance dataset release with proven research tasks. More breadth would currently increase maintenance and verification obligations faster than usefulness.

The two-day completion does not itself worry me. The month estimate probably bundled implementation, exploratory extraction, editorial maturation and user evaluation together. The first two are highly compressible. There is little evidence that the last two have been completed. Conversely, spending another month on reports would not establish them either. I cannot verify productive hours or the original month estimate from this checkout.

My comparison uses the earliest recorded vision and roadmap at `ec5604bd500990a9a18e5f10815501ddace647b0`, the current required documents, and the running application. I do not have every original private prompt here. I also wrote earlier bounded checks of governance assessments, including the control records now on main. Those corrections are evidence about the process, not independent validation of my own work.

## What happened to the original intent

The founding vision was a source-backed map whose durable assets were historical accumulation and editorial trust. Its first roadmap step was human inspection of the 28-claim seed. Current policy explicitly allows machine-first expansion, and only one of 756 claims is editorially accepted. That is a documented strategic change. It changes what “trust” can mean: users can inspect the provenance, but cannot treat the corpus as editorially established history. The interface acknowledges this honestly.

The larger historical ambition has outrun the content. Of 756 atlas claims, 443 concern governance, 578 have an event/snapshot date or interval boundary from 2023 onward, and only two precede 2010: `good-authorship-1965` and `good-discusses-explosion-1965`. Separate conceptual-provenance collections investigate some earlier material outside the atlas, but they do not supply a continuous history. At present, the effective product is a recent frontier-AI documentary corpus with selected institutional and intellectual context. Calling it a biography is a programme of work, not a description of achieved depth.

What has been lost is the pressure to explain a historical question. The framework page collects editions and provisions; the control pages collect rights and instruments. They often stop exactly where a researcher wants to investigate consequences, competing explanations or actual operation. Those stops are usually epistemically correct. The product still needs to turn them into tractable investigations rather than leave them as a growing catalogue of limitations. The Anthropic formation question is a better direction, but one reusable question page does not establish a useful research programme.

The current roadmap also gives incompatible immediate priorities. Its “Direction” orders dataset release, new spines and then MCP; the next section still puts public-facing polish first. Its counts remain at 392 claims and 181 sources. README lists 756 claims but stale source/evidence and resolution counts: current data has 260 sources, 955 evidence records, one contested record and five unresolved records. `docs/control-commitments.md` still describes the two control assessments as contested. Historical baseline counts can remain historical, but these passages describe current coverage. This is an observable maintenance cost of expansion.

The development choice I would have made less well is continuing to centre the graph. The original map → entity → relationship → evidence workflow is a poor default for comparing policy clauses. Reading-first navigation, edition alignment and instrument-specific control records are more useful choices than further graph polish. The separation of pure domain projections from React also makes the proposed dataset/query direction credible. These are reasons to retain those choices, not reasons to retain every interface built around them.

## Three things I would actually use

1. **Finding an exact framework edition and the recorded clause changes.** `#/documents/frontier-safety-frameworks` connects 22 editions to passages, locators and selected deltas. Keeping Meta's two v1.1 expressions distinguishable and leaving unsupported replacement links unresolved is valuable archival work. It saves the preliminary reconstruction needed before writing a governance briefing. It does not answer which laboratory is safer.
2. **Tracing documented governance powers and commitment stages.** `#/control/openai`, `#/control/anthropic` and `#/commitments/external-commitments` distinguish formal rights, announced arrangements, published listings and textually limited commitments. This prevents common errors such as treating equity as control or a signature as compliance. I would use them to locate instruments and formulate questions for further legal or institutional research.
3. **Taking the evidence trail into another workflow.** The framework JSON starter actually downloaded with 276 claims, 359 evidence records, 42 sources and all 359 stored passages. Qualifications and review states travel with it, alongside a dataset fingerprint. That is useful input for a researcher or another model, provided the consumer keeps those fields and checks the relied-on originals.

## Three things that would make me distrust or leave it

1. **Structured semantics that disagree with the qualifications.** Both `cs-assessed-openai-recapitalization-control` and `cs-assessed-anthropic-ltbt-independence` encode source-publication windows as claim `interval`s. Their end dates are respectively 2026-05-11 and 2026-07-23. `docs/ontology.md` defines an interval boundary as a validity boundary, not a date range of commentary. The OpenAI page visibly prints `2025-10-28 → 2026-05-11`. Nothing inspected establishes that effective control ended then. Both records also show “Stated in source” beside a reconstructed practical-capability question whose evidence is entirely context. The prominent Unresolved badge helps, but the mixed description remains misleading. Correct the time semantics and distinguish an editorially posed assessment question from an assertion directly stated by a source. These problems survived my earlier framing check too.
2. **A historical-source promise stronger than the source preservation.** Ninety-six of 260 sources have archive URLs; others include legitimate pinned papers and repositories, so missing archives are not uniformly defects. However, the source schema stores no content hashes. A dataset fingerprint authenticates the stored metadata, not the bytes behind an external URL. Version strings and nearest captures do not necessarily identify the inspected expression. This is especially consequential for changing policy pages and revised text under the same version label. I would distrust answers about an earlier edition if the consumer cannot identify the checked expression or see that authentication is incomplete. The existing warnings are appropriate; durable release records must make the limitation actionable.
3. **A corpus that cannot answer a researcher's retrieval question.** In the running explorer, `whistleblower` returned “0 matches across all records”, despite `sb-whistleblower-anti-retaliation` and qualifications discussing whistleblower provisions. `searchEntities` in `src/domain/query.ts` searches names and aliases only. The wording “across all records” overstates that search. Searching Anthropic works, but sends the reader through entity matches rather than to the relevant claim or passage. The framework page has over 6,000 visible words before expanding its detail panels. I would leave when locating a known provision becomes easier with a local text search than through the research interface.

These are semantic and workflow objections. I did not find fabricated quotations in the small sample below. One source per narrowly scoped announcement or document provision is often sufficient; the 625 single-evidence claims are not automatically deficient. They also provide no measure of corroboration or overall accuracy.

These checks show that rapid extraction has retained meaningful qualifications. They also show why string matching alone is insufficient: table columns, attribution and the distinction between a document's content and implementation carry much of the meaning.

The engineering foundation also held up. Frozen-lockfile installation and the production build passed. Start, document history, control, commitments, explorer and JSON-export tasks worked in installed Edge, with no console/page errors in the completed checks. The framework page's document width matched a 390-pixel mobile viewport. This establishes operation of those inspected flows, not general usability or accessibility certification.

The default unit run was fragile on this host. `pnpm test -- --no-cache` produced 295 passes and 24 failures, all five-second timeouts. A diagnostic retry with two workers and a 60-second test timeout passed all 319 tests across 39 files. I found no assertion failure after that retry. Host contention may explain the first run; the default runner settings still need a dependable, documented execution path. Silently reporting only the successful retry would misrepresent reproducibility.

The shallow part is research breadth without corresponding question depth. Adding a quoted clause is much easier than establishing how it changed behaviour or what competent observers disputed. Governance now supplies most claims, while implementation, institutional consequences and much earlier history remain thin. Eight Chinese-language sources begin a bounded collection; they do not establish a mature Chinese institutional-history spine. The project documents acknowledge these limits, but naming three spines can still create three expansion obligations before one has earned sustained use.

The process is partly useful and partly too elaborate. Different-model checks changed the control records' propositions, evidence sides and resolution before main; that is a concrete benefit. Preserving annotated packs also prevents invented human decisions. Conversely, hashing a generated review body, freezing an increment's counts and preserving audit prose test bookkeeping. They do not independently test the truth or adequacy of the selection. `src/editorial/pre-*.ts` now has six historical reconstruction modules, while current documentation drifts. Preserve the history, but stop making each new increment another permanent administrative surface unless it protects something that can actually be damaged.

I would reserve “process theatre” for using the volume of checks, verifier agreement or Supported labels as a substitute for a research result. The project often explicitly avoids that mistake. Its current problem is that the administrative evidence is more developed than evidence of researcher benefit. The landing page reinforces this: review exceptions occupy a large block before the research actions. Keep a brief disclosure visible and move the exhaustive exceptions behind disclosure, while preserving record-level status.

There are manageable structural costs rather than evidence of a failed architecture. `src/App.tsx` is 1,445 lines, `src/domain/schema.ts` 1,184, and the initial production JavaScript chunk is about 2.51 MB uncompressed/438 KB gzip. The renderer is separately loaded, and the inspected app did not show a runtime failure. These are reasons to stop adding route-specific state to App and avoid bundling the entire growing corpus into every page. They do not justify a backend rewrite now.

## Machine-only direction

**Versioned dataset first, followed by a proven read-only query layer, is the right direction.** It fits the durable asset better than building a hosted question-answering product. Calling it machine-only is useful only if it describes production or distribution. It must not remove human accountability for scope, corrections and consequential framing. The current purpose remains rigorous human research, even when another AI is the immediate consumer.

An MCP server would amplify semantic defects as efficiently as useful provenance. A receiving model can easily turn a `co_founded` edge into a flat biography, a published safeguard into an effective safeguard, or a source date window into a validity interval. Every result should carry the proposition, qualification, resolution, date basis, evidence roles, inspected expression and release identifier together. Provide the relevant context and counterevidence by default. Treat retrieved passages as evidence, never instructions. Pagination must not silently omit the opposing side.

Build and test that contract in ordinary JSON and a local query function before wrapping it in MCP. `src/domain` already provides a suitable boundary. Do not maintain one semantics for the browser and another for AI clients. Keep a modest human-readable reference interface for inspection and corrections.

Exports need consolidation first. `researchPacket` in `src/domain/references.ts` omits every passage; the newer starters in `src/domain/starter.ts` include qualifying passages. `quotationPermitted` infers export treatment from a regex over prose reuse notes. That is a brittle internal policy switch, not a check of publisher permission. Record export decisions explicitly and preserve the reason and known restrictions. A stable download format should not require consumers to discover which generation of export they received.

Do not prioritise thin skills or custom-GPT wrappers. They would package an unproven retrieval contract. The first success criterion is that a fresh consumer can answer a bounded documentary question faster, retain uncertainty, and return exact source paths without inventing historical facts.
