# Antecedent texts candidate pack, 2026-11-02: review note

The candidate pack [`research/prehistory/`](../research/prehistory/scope.md) records what texts written before 1965 say about machine intelligence, with Good's "Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine" (1965) as the boundary. It links each text to the existing propositions it articulates, contests or bears on, across collections, and drafts two new antecedent propositions where a recurring idea had no home. Nothing is admitted; `data/`, the schema, the application and the releases are unchanged. Branch `prehistory-pack` from `main` at 8439f7e (v0.26.1).

- **Prepared by:** Claude family (one drafting session; mechanical checks in [`verification.json`](../research/prehistory/verification.json)).
- **Same-family audit:** a fresh-context Claude agent re-checked 32 of the 38 items (24 random, 8 targeted) against the sources and page images: 18 OK, 14 minor, none material; no record found stretched. All 14 were applied or carried into the qualifications ([`audit.md`](../research/prehistory/audit.md), "Dispositions").
- **Cross-family check:** run by OpenAI Codex on 2026-11-03 against the brief [`cross-family-input-2026-11-02.md`](../research/prehistory/cross-family-input-2026-11-02.md) (Part A blind, Part B direct, Part C retrieval; report: [`cross-family-check-2026-11-02.md`](../research/prehistory/cross-family-check-2026-11-02.md)); the pack's answers: [`cross-family-verdicts-2026-11-02.json`](../research/prehistory/cross-family-verdicts-2026-11-02.json). Adjudicated below.
- **Override rule.** A preparer's adjudication against the checker on an earliest-located role or headline is not admitted on that adjudication alone.
- **State.** 29 records, 8 links, 14 sources; [`verification.json`](../research/prehistory/verification.json) reports no problems (21 quotations matched against retrieved text, 25 read on page images of scans without a usable text layer, 2 paraphrases). Records by text: [`pack.md`](../research/prehistory/pack.md).

## Scope

Texts dated before 1965 that concern machine intelligence, read in primary copies: archive scans (King's College Turing Digital Archive; Internet Archive scans of the 1843 Scientific Memoirs and of the 1872 Erewhon), the Papers Past page of 1865, a JSTOR scan of Science (1960), the publisher's open-access reissue of Cybernetics, and a transcription of the 1923 English R.U.R. made from HathiTrust page images. Each text is recorded only on a proposition whose criterion, as written, it meets. Texts the dataset already records on a proposition are not re-recorded on it (45 such records are listed in `pack.md`).

## Open questions, with recommendations

1. **Admit U01 and U02 as drafted?** Recommended: yes, after the cross-family check, in a new collection (below). U01's criterion follows Lovelace's wording and excludes statements about present use only, which keeps Turing's 1951 concession out of the articulations.
2. **Erewhon's civil war (W04).** Recommended: keep as context. The machines in dispute are not described as superhuman intelligence; reading the criterion to cover them would stretch it.
3. **A third proposition, the technological singularity?** Draft criterion: "The acceleration of technological change is approaching a point, called a singularity or described as a discontinuity, beyond which human affairs as they have been known could not continue or could not be foreseen." Recommended: hold until Ulam (1958) is retrieved; it has no home in the dataset (no proposition names it), and Vinge's texts of 1983 and 1993, already in the dataset, would articulate it.
4. **Lucubratio Ebria as W01's earliest located contesting text.** Recommended: yes if the cross-family check agrees that the passage disputes succession, not only control.
5. **C1 headline.** Recommended: retrieve Jefferson (1949) first, then move the headline once, to whichever is earlier and meets the criterion.
6. **Wiener's Cybernetics dated 1948, attested 2019.** Recommended: admit with the attested date and no headline change; ask Codex or a library for the 1948 printing.
7. **R.U.R.'s Robots as machine intelligence.** The Robots are made from a synthetic living substance. Recommended: admit W01 and B6 as fiction with that qualification; keep U02 as context.
8. **Turing 1950 on U02.** Turing calls "Can machines think?" too meaningless to discuss and predicts a change in usage. Recommended: admit as an articulation with that qualification, unless the cross-family check disagrees.
9. **Samuel 1960 (U01) rests on the indexed abstract.** Recommended: hold until the article is read (Codex list).
10. **AMT/C/11 is undated.** Recommended: admit as undated with 1948 claimed; it cannot hold an earliest-located role.
11. **Kolmogorov's letter, attested 2023.** Recommended: admit with the attested date; seek the 1963 printing of the theses.

## How this becomes a collection

- **Collection.** A new propositions collection, "Antecedent texts: machine intelligence before 1965" (a minor release), holding U01 and U02 (and U03 if decided). Its search note states the period 1843–1964, the venues above and the gaps; its introduction says that the order of texts records no influence.
- **Records on other collections' propositions** (W01, W02, W04, W08, B6, C1, C3, M06a) are admitted into those propositions, so they appear in their own collections. The new collection lists them under a "Related propositions" section rather than holding them, so no collection stretches its period and no record is duplicated. Machine succession and superintelligence already start in 1863; economics starts in 1952 and would need its period widened to 1948 only if the Wiener record is admitted and the 1948 date confirmed.
- **Sequence.** Cross-family check (Codex, which can also do Part C) → adjudication → admission on a data branch with a before-snapshot → the collection frame in `src/reading/proposition-frames.ts` and its file in `src/reading/` → release. If showing records from other collections needs interface work, that is a separate session.
- **Codex retrieval** ([list](../research/prehistory/codex-retrieval.md)): Butler 1863 for U02; Čapek's Czech printing; Turing's NPL report; Samuel 1960; Wiener's 1948 printing; Ulam 1958; Jefferson 1949; Hartree 1949.

### Disagreements for the maintainer

1. **R.U.R. on U02 (A2.4, C2).** The pack records **context**; the checker reads **articulates**. The play calls the Robots machines (Czech p. 12, "živé a inteligentní pracovní stroje") and ascribes them intelligence, and the pack records W01 and B6 for the same beings, so a mechanism-only boundary for U02 needs a stated rule. The pack's reading: they are made from a synthetic living substance, so the play does not state that a machine could think. No earliest-located effect either way (1920 is later than 1863). Both readings are printed under the record. Read with the play's own word for the Robots and the pack's W01 and B6 records, the checker's call for one boundary is supported; the decision sheet recommends counting the Robots as machines on all three propositions, with the synthetic-organism qualification.
2. **C1 headline (A1.4, C4, F-Descartes, EL10).** Held under the override rule. The checker proposes Descartes (1637), qualified: words and tasks done as well as people are said not to show knowledge, which meets C1 for restricted probes; Descartes also proposes two tests he holds to be reliable. The preparer reads Descartes as context, because he describes performances rather than a test and treats behavioural tests as reliable, and proposes Jefferson (1949). The checker's own fallback, if C1 is read to require an unrestricted test, is also Jefferson. Both readings are printed under the C1 row.
